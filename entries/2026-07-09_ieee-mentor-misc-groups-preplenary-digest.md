## Summary (EN)

The smaller 802.11 groups posted ~21 new contributions between May 18 and July 8. The AI Offload SG — the study group formed at the March 2026 plenary to explore Wi-Fi as a transport for AI computation offload — shows its first substantive direction-setting: "Thoughts on AIO directions" (1379, Jerome Henry, Cisco, uploaded July 8) and "AI Offloading meets Location-Based Entertainment" (1139, Chih-Ho Hsu), plus agenda/minutes (1122, 1239). In the WNG SC, "Toward Deterministic Wi-Fi: Requirements & Evolution" (1147, Kazi Huq) feeds the determinism theme that dominates the Wi-Fi 9 definition debate. The AIML SC contributed "Deep Learning based Frame Format Detection for WLAN" (1248, Eunsung Jeon, Samsung) — a PHY-side neural detector for frame formats.

The UCM TIG (unified channel models) posted a methodical trio: a comparison of IEEE TGn and 3GPP TR38.901 channel models (1213, Rainer Strobel, Intel), an optical wireless channel model for 802.11 (1217), and a ray-tracing methods survey for UCM parameterization (1225) — groundwork for evaluation methodology that any future amendment (and any AI-for-Wi-Fi training pipeline) will inherit. TGbq (mmWave) added an on-demand service period proposal for aperiodic downlink traffic (1236, Xiangxin Gu) and transmission-failure handling (1374, Chaoming Luo), alongside its July agenda. ARC SC and WG-level documents (agendas, minutes, an ITU-T SG20 liaison at 1247, a style-guide note) complete the picture.
