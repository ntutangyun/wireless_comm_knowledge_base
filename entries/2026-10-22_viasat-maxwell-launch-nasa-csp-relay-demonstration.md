## Summary (EN)

SatNews reports (21 October 2026) that Viasat launched its Maxwell satellite on Tuesday 20 October 2026 at 7:04 a.m. EDT on the SpaceX Bandwagon-5 rideshare from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. Maxwell was built by Rocket Lab at its Long Beach facility; the satellite (spacecraft bus) provides power, attitude control and propulsion.

The satellite is part of NASA's Communications Services Project (CSP), which aims to validate commercial relay services for government and commercial LEO satellites as NASA plans to retire its legacy TDRS fleet. Viasat holds two CSP awards totaling $81 million, and this is its second CSP demonstration; the first was a launch telemetry milestone on Blue Origin's New Glenn flight in November 2025.

Maxwell carries a space-qualified Ka-band terminal that links to the ViaSat-3 GEO constellation and an L-band terminal using Rocket Lab's Frontier radio, with direct links to Viasat's Real-Time Earth network for S-, X- and Ka-band downlinks. The five-month campaign runs through March 2027 and focuses on real-time, low-latency data transport over Viasat's HaloNet architecture. Viasat Government VP John Reeves is quoted saying the demonstration "will show how new commercial services can transform LEO operations". The article does not give Maxwell's orbit, mass, frequencies, data rates or link performance figures.
