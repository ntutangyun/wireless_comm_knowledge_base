## Summary (EN)

On 2026-09-07 the US Department of Commerce announced that India has joined an initiative of the United States and 24 other countries on the development and deployment of next-generation 6G networks. The agreement followed a bilateral meeting between US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and India's Minister of State for Commerce & Industry and for Electronics & IT (MeitY), Jitin Prasada, held on the sidelines of the G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina (2026-09-01/02). India's delegation included representatives from the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, the Department of Science and Technology, and the DPIIT.

## Summary (ZH)

2026 年 9 月 7 日，美国商务部宣布印度加入一项由美国与另外 24 个国家组成的倡议，共同推进下一代 6G 网络的研发与部署。该协议是在美国商务部长 Howard Lutnick 与印度商工部及电子信息技术部（MeitY）国务部长 Jitin Prasada 于 2026 年 9 月 1-2 日北卡罗来纳州教堂山（Chapel Hill）G20 创新部长级会议期间举行双边会晤后达成的。印度代表团成员来自电子信息技术部、科学技术部及工业和内部贸易促进部（DPIIT）。

## Key technical points (EN)

- Participants: the United States plus 24 other nations, including India as the newest member (the article does not name the full list of the other 23 countries).
- Stated purpose: ensure future 6G networks "reflect our shared security interests, strengthen our competitiveness, and drive innovation in 6G."
- India's stated goal: strengthen its role in global telecom standard-setting and promote indigenous 6G/5G-Advanced capability across startups, industry, and academia.
- No 6G radio-technology specifications (spectrum bands, waveform, air-interface targets) were disclosed — this is a policy/standards-alignment agreement, not a technical roadmap.
- This is separate from, and additive to, India's own Bharat 6G Alliance (already covered in the KB, 2026-08-27), which is a domestic industry-academia body rather than an international government-to-government initiative.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This is a distinct development from the KB's existing Bharat 6G Alliance coverage: it is a government-to-government standards-and-security alignment agreement rather than a domestic research consortium, and it puts India inside the same US-anchored 6G policy bloc as (per the alliance's known prior membership, not itemized in this specific announcement) other major economies. For a KB tracking the international politics of 6G standard-setting alongside the technical work, this is a fresh, dated data point about which countries are aligning on 6G governance ahead of 3GPP/ITU technical work — distinct from, and not a duplicate of, either the Bharat 6G Alliance entry or the IIT Madras THz testbed entry already on file.
