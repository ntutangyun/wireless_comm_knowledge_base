## Summary (EN)
Telesat and antenna-technology startup Farcast announced (2026-09-02) the completion of an over-the-air demonstration milestone for Telesat's planned Lightspeed LEO constellation: an end-to-end satellite link was established and maintained through a user terminal built around Farcast's electronically steered antenna (ESA), using Telesat's LEO 3 test satellite. The sourced differentiator is the **shared aperture** — the terminal transmits and receives through a single antenna panel rather than separate transmit and receive apertures. Measured link performance closely matched predicted link budgets, which both companies describe as validating the underlying terminal technology. The release's only claim about the architecture's benefit is that it "reduces hardware complexity without compromising performance" and "significantly lowers size, weight, power and cost (SWaP-C)"; it names no baseline architecture for that comparison. The companies frame this as a step toward a fully integrated Lightspeed user terminal combining Farcast's antenna platform with Telesat's modem, following roughly four years of joint development; no terminal cost, throughput, or production-timeline figures were disclosed.

## Key technical points (EN)
- **What was demonstrated:** an end-to-end satellite link over Telesat's LEO 3 test satellite, sustained through a shared-aperture user terminal integrating Farcast's ESA — a functional over-the-air validation, not a lab/simulation-only result.
- **Performance:** measured results "closely matched predicted link budgets" — a qualitative validation claim; no numeric throughput, EIRP, or G/T figures were disclosed.
- **Antenna architecture:** a shared aperture, transmitting and receiving through one panel. The release states only that this "reduces hardware complexity without compromising performance" and "significantly lowers size, weight, power and cost (SWaP-C)" — no comparator architecture is named, and the release does not describe the design as phased-array or proprietary.
- **Programme context:** roughly four years of Telesat/Farcast joint development preceded this milestone; next phase is a fully integrated user terminal pairing Farcast's antenna with Telesat's own modem. SpaceNews reports the Lightspeed Pathfinder launch as targeted for around December 2026 — that date comes from SpaceNews, not from any prior entry in this KB (the 08-05 Telesat entry gives only "SpaceX launches over ~1 year from mid-2026" and service from Q4 2027).
