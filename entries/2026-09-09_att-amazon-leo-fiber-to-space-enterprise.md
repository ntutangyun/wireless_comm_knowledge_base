## Summary (EN)
AT&T and Amazon Leo (the commercial arm of Amazon's Project Kuiper constellation) published new architectural and operational detail on their "fiber-to-space" alliance, first announced as a strategic partnership in February 2026 (per SatNews, 2026-02-04). The September update describes Ku/Ka-band enterprise terminals using electronically steered phased-array antennas that connect to local AWS cloud regions via AT&T's high-capacity fiber network, with the stated aim of routing enterprise/cloud traffic directly rather than through public-internet hops. The architecture also includes an automated failover capability tied to AT&T's FirstNet public-safety network, intended to maintain connectivity during ground-infrastructure outages. AT&T frames the target market as high-margin corporate, government, and industrial connectivity -- competing on integrated fiber-satellite service delivery rather than on raw satellite count against SpaceX Starlink -- and cites that cellular subscribers are outside terrestrial coverage roughly two percent of the time, predominantly in unserved wilderness areas, national parks or maritime environments, per AT&T CEO John Stankey, as the addressable use case. The update also references Globalsat Group's previously announced (March 2026, per SatNews 2026-03-18) reseller agreement distributing Amazon Leo services across the Americas. Regulatory context: Amazon's Gen1 FCC authorisation set a milestone of 1,616 satellites by 30 July 2026 (3,232 by 2029); the FCC granted a conditional waiver of that milestone on 5 June 2026 (Order DA 26-553), with roughly 700 satellites projected by the original date. The cited article restates the milestone as 1,618 of 3,236 without noting the waiver. Launch capacity is split across ULA, Arianespace, and SpaceX.

## Key technical points (EN)
- **Terminal:** Ku/Ka-band enterprise terminal with electronically steered phased-array antenna (distinct from consumer flat-panel terminals).
- **Routing architecture:** satellite gateway traffic connects to local AWS cloud regions via AT&T's fiber backbone, described as bypassing public-internet hops for enterprise/cloud traffic.
- **Resilience feature:** automated failover integrated with AT&T's FirstNet public-safety network during ground-infrastructure outages.
- **Regulatory context:** Amazon's Gen1 FCC authorisation set a milestone of 1,616 satellites by 30 July 2026 (3,232 by 2029); the FCC granted a conditional waiver of that milestone on 5 June 2026 (Order DA 26-553), with roughly 700 satellites projected by the original date. The cited article restates the milestone as 1,618 of 3,236 without noting the waiver. Launch capacity split across ULA/Arianespace/SpaceX.
- **Distribution:** Globalsat Group reseller agreement (announced March 2026, per SatNews 2026-03-18) covers Amazon Leo service distribution across the Americas.
- **Positioning:** framed as targeting high-margin enterprise/government/industrial connectivity rather than competing on raw satellite count with Starlink; cites ~2% terrestrial-coverage-gap time for cellular subscribers — predominantly unserved wilderness areas, national parks or maritime environments, per AT&T CEO John Stankey — as the addressable market.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
This KB's D2D/enterprise-satellite thread has tracked Amazon Leo's constellation deployment and FCC filings, but this is the first entry describing the concrete terminal and network architecture behind the AT&T partnership rather than its business terms: a phased-array (not flat-panel) enterprise terminal, a named cloud-routing path (satellite gateway to AWS region via AT&T fiber, bypassing the public internet), and a public-safety-network failover tie-in via FirstNet. It reflects a distinct competitive axis from the direct-to-cell/direct-to-device entrants already tracked in this bin (Starlink, AST SpaceMobile, Equatys, Elveo) -- enterprise/cloud backhaul integration rather than consumer or unmodified-phone connectivity.
