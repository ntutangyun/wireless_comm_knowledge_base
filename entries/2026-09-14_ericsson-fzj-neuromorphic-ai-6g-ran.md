## Summary (EN)

Ericsson announced a research collaboration with Forschungszentrum Jülich (FZJ), the German national research centre operating JUPITER — Europe's first exascale supercomputer — to develop AI methods for the core network, network management, and RAN across the 5G-to-6G transition, with a specific focus on energy efficiency. The work spans three areas: (1) systematic benchmarking of AI approaches across Ericsson's portfolio for execution speed, scalability, information retention, and storage efficiency, with JUPITER available for large-scale model training where security and commercial conditions allow; (2) energy-efficient computing for AI inference at the radio and edge, explicitly including neuromorphic computing approaches — such as memristor-based hardware — to speed up optimization and reduce energy use versus classical methods; and (3) research into Modular Supercomputing Architecture concepts and operational strategies (e.g., heat recovery) to improve energy efficiency in the HPC and cloud infrastructure.
