## Summary (EN)
SatNews reported 31 August 2026 (corroborated by an independent web search summary) that SpaceX has filed a petition with the FCC asking regulators to condition — or block outright — a pending geostationary (GSO) satellite authorization sought by Viasat. SpaceX argues that Viasat's planned radio-frequency allocation and orbital configuration, operating in overlapping Ku-band (12/14 GHz) and Ka-band (20/30 GHz) allocations, would create uncoordinated transmissions that exceed statutory Equivalent Power Flux-Density (EPFD) thresholds and degrade downlink throughput at Starlink LEO ground terminals. The filing requests three specific mitigations if the FCC proceeds with authorization: (1) strict power caps on the shared frequencies, (2) mandatory beam-steering with physical slant-angle masking so Viasat's spot beams cannot illuminate active Starlink terminals during low-elevation passes, and (3) an automatic cease-operation provision written into Viasat's license, triggering immediate signal mitigation if harmful interference is demonstrated. Coverage available at scan time did not surface a specific docket or filing number, numerical EPFD threshold values, or direct quotations from the petition text; the article and corroborating search summary describe the substance but not the primary document.

The notable structural point is the role reversal: Viasat has historically been the party filing petitions to deny or condition SpaceX/Starlink authorizations on interference grounds (including environmental-review requests covered elsewhere in trade press); here SpaceX is the petitioner against a Viasat GSO deployment, framing the LEO megaconstellation as the incumbent whose ground terminals need protecting from a new GSO entrant rather than the other way around.
