## Summary (EN)

On 9 September 2026 (Rockville, Md. and Seoul), Ceva, Inc. and LG Electronics announced a partnership pairing Ceva's Ceva-Waves UWB baseband IP and software with UWB radio-frequency (RF) technology developed by LG's SoC Center, giving semiconductor and OEM customers a combined, silicon-ready UWB platform for automotive, industrial and consumer SoCs. LG's UWB RF supports TSMC's 22nm process. The companies state that a major U.S. semiconductor company has already been secured as a customer for the combined solution, which the release frames as commercial validation for the joint offering.

Ceva's contribution is the same Ceva-Waves baseband/software stack the KB already tracks (2026-05-26_ceva-waves-uwb-ip-802154ab, launched 10 March 2026): IEEE 802.15.4ab support, non-line-of-sight ranging performance, extended range, radar sensing, support for the new channels introduced in the next-generation standard, low power and interference resilience. What is new here is the RF half — previously a gap licensees had to fill themselves — now paired end-to-end with LG's 22nm-capable RF front-end, plus a named category of customer (a major U.S. semiconductor company) already adopting the joint stack. The release cites ABI Research's forecast of nearly 1.18 billion UWB device shipments by 2030, up from 597 million in 2026, as market context. Executives quoted: KANG YongSeok (VP, LG Electronics) and Tal Shalev (VP/GM, Ceva).

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
This closes the RF gap in the KB's existing Ceva-Waves coverage: the March 2026 entry (2026-05-26_ceva-waves-uwb-ip-802154ab) was baseband/software IP only, leaving licensees to source or design their own 802.15.4ab-capable RF front-end. Pairing it with LG's TSMC-22nm RF technology turns the offering into an end-to-end, silicon-ready UWB platform, and the disclosed anchor-customer licensing is the first concrete commercial-adoption signal the KB has recorded for a 4ab IP stack (as opposed to a finished SoC like ST's ST64UWB). It is a distinct event from the March announcement — a different partner, a different (RF) half of the stack, and a new customer-adoption data point — not a re-tread.
