## Summary (EN)
On 17 August 2026 (transaction closed 14 August after all US and foreign regulatory approvals), Lynk Global and Omnispace announced completion of the merger first announced in October 2025, forming a single direct-to-device (D2D) operator named **Elveo Mobile**, headquartered in the Washington, D.C. area and led by CEO Ramu Potarazu. SES — which had been a strategic shareholder of Lynk and had already flown two MEO pathfinder satellites carrying an in-orbit relay capability compatible with the Elveo network — remains a leading shareholder, alongside Fortress, StepStone Group, Columbia Capital, Telcom Ventures and Blazar Ventures; Guggenheim Securities advised. Funding amounts were not disclosed.

The technical proposition combines the two predecessors' assets. Omnispace contributes **60 MHz of globally coordinated S-band mobile-satellite spectrum in 3GPP bands n255 / n256** — the 2 GHz MSS allocation on which its planned 600+-satellite constellation had stalled after it reported interference from SpaceX operations in the US. Lynk contributes its operating heritage: seven small LEO satellites already providing intermittent SMS / alert service (mainly to island-nation MNOs) using terrestrial cellular frequencies borrowed from partner operators, and a book of contractual agreements with **more than 50 mobile network operators across 60+ countries** covering markets of over one billion people. Elveo describes its next-generation payload as carrying **on-board processing with adaptive digital beamforming and integrated edge computing** so that capacity can be reconfigured on orbit — "the network will be able to process and adapt on orbit" — supporting 2G, 4G and 5G protocols to unmodified smartphones and IoT devices for voice, SMS and broadband data, across consumer, enterprise, automotive and defense segments.

On timeline, the company says integration into LEO manufacturing runs through late 2026 and 2027, with continuous global D2D service targeted for **2027**, using SES GEO and MEO satellites as a relay/backhaul layer during and after LEO build-out; partnership, manufacturing and launch announcements are promised. The article frames the competitive field as three-way: Starlink direct-to-cell (650+ satellites in orbit) and AST SpaceMobile as the two other scaled D2D contenders.

## Key technical points (EN)
- **Merger closed 14 Aug 2026**, announced 17 Aug; new entity Elveo Mobile (Washington, D.C. area; CEO Ramu Potarazu); Lynk + Omnispace deal first announced Oct 2025.
- **Spectrum:** 60 MHz of globally coordinated S-band MSS in 3GPP **n255 / n256** (Omnispace's 2 GHz MSS holdings) — licensed-MSS route, in contrast to Lynk's borrowed-terrestrial-band and Starlink's partner-MNO-band models.
- **In-orbit assets today:** 7 Lynk small LEO satellites giving intermittent messaging / alerts; Omnispace's 600+-satellite plan had stalled after reported interference from SpaceX operations in the US.
- **Next-gen payload:** on-board processing, adaptive digital beamforming, integrated edge computing; 2G/4G/5G to unmodified phones and IoT; voice, SMS, broadband data.
- **Commercial book:** 50+ MNO agreements, 60+ countries, >1 billion people covered by partner markets.
- **Architecture roadmap:** SES GEO/MEO satellites as relay layer (two MEO pathfinders already flown); LEO manufacturing integration late 2026–2027; continuous global D2D targeted 2027.
- **Investors:** SES (leading shareholder), Fortress, StepStone, Columbia Capital, Telcom Ventures, Blazar Ventures; advisor Guggenheim; amounts undisclosed.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
The KB's direct-to-device thread has been dominated by three architectures: Starlink's partner-MNO-band direct-to-cell (2026-07-03 Starlink Mobile, 2026-08-17 Starlink PoP study), AST SpaceMobile's large-array Block-2 approach (2026-08-05 Rakuten Japan live, 2026-08-07 BlueBird 11–13, 2026-08-15 Q2 update), and the emerging neutral-host / MSS-spectrum consortia (2026-08-14 Space42 + Viasat Equatys, 2026-05-28 EU 2 GHz MSS three-way split, 2026-07-22 Amazon–Globalstar). Elveo Mobile is new on two counts. First, it is the first entry documenting the Lynk–Omnispace combination as a *closed* transaction with concrete architecture: a **licensed 2 GHz S-band MSS (n255/n256) LEO D2D operator** with on-orbit processing and digital beamforming — the same n255/n256 band family that Keysight/Samsung tested for Rel-19 NR-NTN (2026-05-30) and that the EU 2 GHz MSS decision reallocated (2026-05-28), so this is the commercial counterpart of that spectrum thread. Second, it introduces a **multi-orbit relay topology** (SES GEO + MEO as backhaul for a thin LEO layer) as the stated path to "continuous" coverage before the LEO fleet is dense — an architectural contrast to the pure-LEO density race and directly relevant to the multi-orbit handover work in 2026-08-17_arxiv-marl-multi-orbit-handover-power. Watch items: the interference dispute that stalled Omnispace's constellation is not described as resolved, the LEO manufacturer/launch provider is not named, and the 2027 continuous-service target should be treated as an announced-future-date event until a landing is verifiable.
