## Summary (EN)
This entry rests mainly on one secondary source: the first item of a Wi-Fi NOW news roundup (author Claus Hetting, published 24 November 2026). Ofcom's statement page (linked from the roundup) returned HTTP 403; a LexisNexis legal-news summary dated 17 November 2026 independently confirms the date, the 88 areas and the 156 fixed links. Figures below are Wi-Fi NOW's reporting of Ofcom's announcement unless attributed to LexisNexis.

According to the roundup, Ofcom confirmed on 17 November that future mobile licences in the upper 6 GHz band (6585-7125 MHz, where, per Wi-Fi NOW, mobile services will have priority under Ofcom's spectrum-sharing framework) will initially be restricted to 88 high-density areas, primarily major urban centres. The designated areas are expected to be cleared for mobile deployment by 2032, with some services potentially launching from 2030 (LexisNexis, 17 Nov: deployment possible across all areas from February 2032; earlier deployment after an award planned for 2029 or 2030, no earlier than 1 January 2030). Ofcom does not expect to authorise high-power mobile deployment elsewhere before 1 January 2035, pending further evidence of demand. Ofcom will also begin the statutory process to revoke licences covering 156 fixed links (Wi-Fi NOW: '156 existing fixed-link licences'), with a five-year notice period according to LexisNexis, because they could interfere with future mobile operations. The roundup adds that the decision follows Ofcom's earlier allocation of an additional 160 MHz for licence-exempt Wi-Fi, and that the EU "continues to be undecided" on its allocation of the upper 6 GHz band.
