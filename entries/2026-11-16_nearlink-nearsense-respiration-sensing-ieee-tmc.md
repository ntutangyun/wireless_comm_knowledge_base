## Summary (EN)

"NearSense: Exploring NearLink for New-Generation Wireless Sensing" (Zijun Han, Xuanzhi Wang, Yang Li, Dan Wu, Hongliu Yang, Wanru Ning, Zhiyun Yao, Wenwei Li, Xingqing Cheng, Zixiang Ma, Daqing Zhang) appears in IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing, vol. 25, no. 7, pp. 9863-9877 (July 2026; DOI 10.1109/TMC.2026.3655026). The authors state that NearLink-based wireless sensing "has not yet been explored" and present their work as the first to explore NearLink's sensing potential. They examine two questions: how NearLink's communication-oriented signals can be adapted for sensing, and how sensing performance can be kept up under multipath interference and low-power constraints. They build a prototype, NearSense, and use respiration detection as a case study, reporting an average detection rate of 98% and a false-alarm rate below 1.5% under various real-life interference conditions.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's NearLink academic coverage so far is a communication-side SLE-vs-BLE measurement study and the alliance's own sensing/ranging features. This is a peer-reviewed journal paper from a Peking University team with Huawei co-authors (Crossref lists two authors at Huawei's Wireless Technology Lab), taking the NearLink link itself as a sensing signal source, the same repurposing idea long applied to Wi-Fi. Priority is as claimed by the authors.
