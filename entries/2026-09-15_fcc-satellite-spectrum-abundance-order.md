## Summary (EN)
On 2026-09-08, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr announced in a news release that the Commission will vote on two items at its September Open Meeting — since confirmed by the FCC's own tentative agenda for September 30, 2026 — one of which is a Satellite Spectrum Abundance Report and Order (the other is an unrelated UWB Part 15 rules-overhaul NPRM, already covered separately in this KB). The draft Report and Order, if adopted, would unlock more than 1,000 MHz of spectrum in the 12.7 GHz and 42 GHz bands for satellite use, bringing more capacity for satellite broadband to the home, for in-flight and on-ship connectivity, and for core traffic-routing functions in satellite ground networks. The release frames this Order as part of a broader package of actions that, together, would also bolster spectrum for fixed earth stations, inter-satellite links, and telemetry/tracking/control (TT&C), though those items are not specific to the 12.7/42 GHz bands themselves. Alongside the Order, the Commission is circulating a companion Spectrum Abundance Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (FNPRM) exploring freeing up an additional 1,450 MHz of spectrum in the Ku- and Ka-bands, together with 138.25 GHz in the D-band, for more intensive satellite communications use. A second FNPRM, described by the FCC as covering "weird space stuff" (WSS), adds further candidate bands under consideration for non-connectivity space missions such as in-orbit servicing, refueling, and manufacturing.

## Key technical points (EN)
- **Satellite Spectrum Abundance Report and Order (draft, announced 2026-09-08, vote scheduled 2026-09-30 per the FCC's tentative agenda for that meeting):** unlocks more than 1,000 MHz across the 12.7 GHz and 42 GHz bands for satellite use — the news release specifically credits this to bringing more capacity for consumer-terminal home broadband, in-flight/on-ship connectivity, and core ground traffic-routing; fixed earth stations, earth stations in motion, and inter-satellite links are named elsewhere in the release as benefiting from the broader package of actions in the Order, not specifically from the 12.7/42 GHz allocation.
- **Companion Spectrum Abundance FNPRM:** seeks comment on freeing up an additional 1,450 MHz in the Ku- and Ka-bands, plus 138.25 GHz in the D-band, for more intensive satellite communications use.
- **WSS (Weird Space Stuff) FNPRM:** adds further candidate spectrum bands for next-generation, non-connectivity space missions — in-orbit servicing, refueling, manufacturing, and operations on or around the Moon.
- **Status:** these are draft items circulated for the Commissioners' review; the FCC's own language is "these final rules, if adopted" — the order has not yet been voted on. The FCC's 2026-09-08 news release said public drafts would be posted on FCC.gov the next day; the September Open Meeting vote is confirmed for 2026-09-30 by the FCC's own tentative agenda for that meeting.
- **Distinct from prior KB coverage:** this is a separate proceeding from the 2026-07-11 / 2026-08-05 FCC Part 100 satellite-licensing modernization order (which addressed licensing process, not new spectrum band allocations) and from the 2026-08-07 unlicensed direct-to-device NPRM (which let unlicensed terrestrial Part 15 devices communicate directly with satellites in the 902–928 MHz, 2400–2483.5 MHz, and 5725–5850 MHz bands, not satellite-side Ku/Ka/D-band allocations).

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
Where earlier satellite-regulation entries in this KB covered licensing-process changes (the FCC's Part 100 space modernization order), a regional split of an existing MSS band (the EU 2 GHz proposal) or disputes over a single authorization, this item is about opening additional spectrum to satellite services in the US. According to the FCC's release, the 12.7 GHz and 42 GHz changes are aimed at home broadband, in-flight and on-ship connectivity and ground traffic routing, while the wider package of actions also addresses inter-satellite links, TT&C and fixed earth stations. It is still a circulated draft: the Commission is scheduled to vote on 2026-09-30, so this entry records the proposal, not an adopted rule; what the adopted text contains can only be confirmed after that vote.
