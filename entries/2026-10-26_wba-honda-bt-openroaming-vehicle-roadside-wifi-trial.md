## Summary (EN)

A Honda Motor Europe member release published on the Wireless Broadband Alliance (WBA) website, dated 6 October 2026, describes a trial by Honda R&D Europe UK (HRE-UK) and BT, supported by the WBA, of how trusted public Wi-Fi could complement cellular connectivity for vehicles in a live UK roadside environment. The release says the teams demonstrated how trusted public Wi-Fi could complement cellular connectivity for vehicles, testing OpenRoaming (which lets devices authenticate and connect automatically to participating networks) end-to-end without requiring driver interaction, and describes the work as a first-of-its-kind trial and an "industry-first automotive OpenRoaming roadside demonstration in the UK" (the wording is the participants' own claim as quoted in the article).

The article assigns roles as follows. The WBA provided access to its OpenRoaming federation and the wider Wi-Fi identity ecosystem. BT configured and deployed the roadside Wi-Fi and network capabilities. HRE-UK adapted and instrumented the vehicle side, using a specially equipped test vehicle, with Beam Connectivity named as supporting the trial. Testing began in the lab and then moved to on-street locations, where the teams evaluated connectivity, authentication, coverage and connection while the vehicle was parked or moving and at different distances from the Wi-Fi hotspots.

The article gives no throughput, latency or handover figures. It states that the initial findings are "very encouraging" while the work remains at the development and trial stage, that BT's research had identified a growing connectivity requirement for data-intensive vehicles particularly when parked, and that the stated use case is delivering software updates where vehicles naturally stop, which the participants suggest could extend to driveways, car parks, offices, petrol stations and EV charge points. HRE-UK and its partners say they will continue to explore OpenRoaming as part of Honda's open-innovation work.
