## Summary (EN)

AppleInsider (Andrew Orr, Oct 14, 2026) reports that the new Siri Remote, introduced alongside the fourth-generation Apple TV, adds Precision Finding through Find My, plus a built-in tone inducer that can play a sound when the remote goes missing. According to Apple's footnotes cited by the article, Precision Finding requires an iPhone Duo, iPhone Air, or iPhone 17 or later (excluding the iPhone 17e), running the latest iOS, with availability varying by region. The iPhone 15 lineup and most iPhone 16 models, which have a second-generation Ultra Wideband chip and support expanded Precision Finding with AirTag 2, cannot use it with the new remote.

The article says Apple is not using Ultra Wideband here, but a feature recently added to the Bluetooth spec. It states that Bluetooth 6 includes Channel Sounding, "which is likely what Apple is utilizing" - so the attribution is the author's inference, not an Apple statement. It describes Channel Sounding as combining Phase-Based Ranging (PBR), which measures distance from radio signal phases across multiple frequencies, with Round-Trip Timing (RTT), which times signals travelling between devices, giving centimeter-level precision and mimicking UWB functionality at lower cost since dedicated hardware is not required. Older iPhones can still use the existing remote finder (since iOS 17), which shows proximity indicators, but the sound-playing option is exclusive to the fourth-generation Siri Remote.
