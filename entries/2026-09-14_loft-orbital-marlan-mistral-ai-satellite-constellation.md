## Summary (EN)
Announced 2026-09-09 at the Paris International Space Summit, satellite-infrastructure provider Loft Orbital and Marlan Space finalized a $1 billion agreement to expand their Orbitworks joint venture's planned LEO constellation — named **Altair** — from 10 to 50 satellites, with onboard AI models supplied by French AI firm Mistral AI. The cited source (Via Satellite) describes Altair as an Earth Observation (EO) constellation "for sovereign intelligence" running "agentic AI workflows in space", and describes Marlan Space as "a UAE-based investor and subsidiary of Dubai conglomerate IHC" — note that other reporting (The National, 2026-09-08) places Marlan Space in Abu Dhabi and IHC as an Abu Dhabi holding company; the two accounts conflict on the emirate and are left unreconciled here. The National also reports that IHC announced it would acquire an 80% stake in Marlan Space (announced, not confirmed as completed), and that Orbitworks' Abu Dhabi (KEZAD) factory is intended to build up to 50 satellites per year — neither figure appears in the Via Satellite piece. Rather than downlinking raw sensor data for ground processing, Mistral AI models run onboard to process observations at the point of capture, targeting maritime domain awareness, wildfire detection, disaster response, and critical-infrastructure/port security for government, civil, and commercial customers in France, wider Europe, and the UAE. The satellites carry a mix of radar, optical, and other sensors, and BlackSky is named as a Gen-3 optical-imagery satellite contributor. Launch reporting conflicts: Via Satellite has the first 10 satellites launching in October 2026 with MaiaSpace (an ArianeGroup subsidiary) as preferred launch provider for the buildout, while tbreak reports first launches on SpaceX Falcon 9 in 2027. No satellite mass, bus type, or onboard-compute hardware specifications were disclosed in the announcement.

## Key technical points (EN)
- **Constellation scale-up:** the Altair constellation grows from 10 to 50 satellites under the $1B deal, produced in Abu Dhabi. (Production capacity of "up to 50 satellites per year" at the KEZAD factory is reported by The National, not by the cited Via Satellite piece.)
- **Onboard AI:** Mistral AI models run onboard rather than downlinking raw data for ground-based processing — Via Satellite frames this as "agentic AI workflows in space" for an EO constellation serving "sovereign intelligence". (This knowledge base's framing: an orbital edge-inference architecture; the source itself uses neither "edge computing" nor "tip-and-cue".)
- **Sensor mix:** radar, optical, and other sensor types across the fleet (heterogeneous payload, not a single-sensor-type constellation); BlackSky contributes Gen-3 optical imagery satellites.
- **Corporate structure:** IHC announced an 80% acquisition of Marlan Space on 2026-09-08 (The National) — announced, not confirmed complete. No source links that transaction causally to the $1B figure, and this entry asserts no such link. The two sources also disagree on whether Marlan Space / IHC are Dubai- or Abu-Dhabi-based.
- **Launch plan (conflicting):** Via Satellite — first 10 satellites targeted for October 2026, MaiaSpace (ArianeGroup) preferred launch provider for the wider buildout; tbreak — first launches on SpaceX Falcon 9 in 2027. No detailed manifest given by either.
- **Disclosure gaps (flagged, not found in public reporting as of this entry):** satellite mass/bus type, onboard compute hardware (accelerator type, power budget), specific Mistral model(s) or model size deployed on orbit, and funding structure/equity split behind the $1B figure.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
This is the KB's first entry describing a named foundation-model company's models running as the onboard inference stack of a satellite constellation, rather than a bespoke onboard ML model built in-house by a satellite operator (the KB's existing sat-ai entries cover onboard anomaly detection, beam-hopping schedulers, and GNSS/PNT security models, all operator- or research-group-built). Against the KB's existing orbital-compute entries (06-13 space data centers, 08-18 orbital AI carbon tradeoffs, 08-27 SpaceX Starmind), Altair adds a commercial, non-SpaceX entrant to that framing. It also ties directly into the same 2026-09-09 Paris International Space Summit that produced this KB's 09-11 Eutelsat/Skynopy ground-network entry — both stories emerged from the same event but describe distinct layers of the stack (space-segment AI compute vs. ground-segment software-defined networking).
