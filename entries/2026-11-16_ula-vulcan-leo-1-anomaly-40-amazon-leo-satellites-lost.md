## Summary (EN)

SatNews (15 Nov 2026) reports that ULA's Vulcan Centaur, flying as Vulcan Leo 1 (LV-01) from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral at 12:40 p.m. ET on 15 November, did not reach low Earth orbit and that all 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites aboard were lost. ULA said the vehicle "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit." The flight was Vulcan's return to flight after a nine-month stand-down and the first Vulcan mission for Amazon Leo; it follows the earlier 3 November KB entry that gave 13 November as the target date (the launch eventually flew on the 15th).

According to the SatNews article, the solid boosters appeared to perform normally, and ULA said it had no specific data yet. The cause has not been made public.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Payload: 40 Amazon Leo satellites; before the flight the constellation had roughly 400 satellites in orbit from 15 launches on Atlas V, Falcon 9 and Ariane 6 (SatNews).
- ULA statement posted about 50 minutes after liftoff: "Since the anomaly just occurred, we don't have any specific data yet to understand what occurred today."
- The U.S. Space Force had paused national security launches on Vulcan pending corrective upgrades.
- Not covered by the sources: FCC milestone consequences for Amazon Leo; the root cause.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This is the outcome of the 13 November Vulcan return-to-flight target tracked in the KB's 3 November entry: instead of adding 40 satellites, the mission added none. The cause is not yet established.
