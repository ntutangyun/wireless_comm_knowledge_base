## Summary (EN)

On 14 September 2026 Verizon announced that nine companies — Amazon Web Services, Cisco, Intel, Keysight Technologies, MediaTek, NVIDIA, Palo Alto Networks, Rohde & Schwarz and VIAVI Solutions — have joined the Verizon 6G Innovation Forum (6GIF), which was launched the previous year with founding members Ericsson, Samsung, Nokia, Meta and Qualcomm Technologies. The release describes the forum's remit as trialling new use cases, testing new spectrum bands and bandwidths, and keeping aligned with standards bodies such as 3GPP, and characterises the new members as adding cloud services, silicon and accelerated computing, automated test emulation, network security and device graphics simulation. Verizon CTO Yago Tenorio frames the 6G objective as "fundamentally bottom-up and use-case-driven", with AI workloads, AI-enabled wearables such as untethered smart glasses, and Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) named as the design targets.

The substantive content is two ISAC field trials run on 5G-Advanced capabilities with founding members. With Samsung, at an international soccer celebration in Dallas, Texas, a single 5G base station using 40 MHz of CBRS spectrum on a virtualised RAN (vRAN) tracked the raw radio variations from six localised Samsung Galaxy smartphones; Samsung's AI RAN and edge-AI platform, Network in a Server (NIS), analysed those ambient signal changes to generate real-time crowd-density heatmaps displayed on commercial phones and a tablet. The release describes this as "the industry's first AI-powered ISAC trial on a virtualized Radio Access Network (vRAN) for crowd detection" (Verizon's own claim) and notes in a footnote that the demo measures crowd density only — no individuals or objects are identified. With Qualcomm, at Qualcomm's San Diego campus, a system using 100 to 400 MHz of millimetre-wave spectrum and four synchronised Transmission and Reception Points (TRPs) mapped and tracked the position and velocity of an airborne target and moving ground vehicles while simultaneously providing 5G Standalone (SA) mobile communications to a phone user on the ground. The release also shows a prototype "AI Sports Companion" application on Meta AI glasses served from the Verizon Edge Network with a locally optimised Small Language Model on an NVIDIA DGX Spark and SportsDataIO statistics, used to motivate the claim that smart glasses will need ultra-low latency and "a more balanced uplink-downlink model". Verizon says it views the 2028 Los Angeles Summer Olympics as "the ultimate interim proving ground on the road to commercial 6G deployment", with a dedicated 6G Lab already established in Los Angeles.

## Key technical points (EN)

- 6GIF membership: nine new members (AWS, Cisco, Intel, Keysight, MediaTek, NVIDIA, Palo Alto Networks, Rohde & Schwarz, VIAVI) join founding members Ericsson, Samsung, Nokia, Meta and Qualcomm Technologies.
- Samsung crowd-density trial: one 5G base station, 40 MHz of CBRS spectrum, vRAN; radio variations from six Samsung Galaxy phones analysed by Samsung's Network in a Server (NIS) AI RAN / edge-AI platform into real-time crowd-density heatmaps; density only, no identification of individuals (release footnote).
- Qualcomm drone/vehicle trial: 100–400 MHz of millimetre-wave spectrum, four synchronised TRPs; position and velocity of an airborne target and ground vehicles tracked while 5G SA communications continued to a ground phone user.
- Both trials are described as using 5G-Advanced capabilities, positioned as the path to evolve ISAC into 6G.
- AI Sports Companion prototype: Meta AI glasses, Verizon Edge Network, locally optimised SLM on an NVIDIA DGX Spark, SportsDataIO data; cited to argue that glasses generate as much uplink as downlink.
- Roadmap statement: the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics as an interim proving ground; a dedicated Verizon 6G Lab in Los Angeles.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The value of this release is that it puts concrete radio parameters on two operator-run ISAC demonstrations — bandwidth, band, site count and the sensing target in each case — where most 6G-forum announcements stop at membership lists. The Samsung trial is notable for doing crowd sensing on a virtualised RAN with commercial phones as the only "sensed" hardware, and the Qualcomm trial for keeping 5G SA service running to a user during multi-TRP tracking. The release's superlative about the Samsung trial is Verizon's own framing and is relayed as such. The membership expansion, the LA 2028 target and the LA 6G Lab are roadmap statements rather than technical results.
