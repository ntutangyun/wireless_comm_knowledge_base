## Summary (EN)

The FCC **adopted** its Space Modernization Order on July 22, 2026 (Report and Order + Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking), formally replacing the decades-old **Part 25** space and earth-station rules with a new **Part 100** — the vote flagged in 2026-07-11_fcc-part100-space-modernization-order has now landed, confirmed across multiple law-firm analyses (Covington, Holland & Knight, National Law Review, late July). Headline mechanics of the adopted order: license terms extend to **20 years** for most space and earth stations; processing rounds become **voluntary (opt-in), annual and band-specific**, with a filing window running **January 1 – October 31** and initial rounds opening for Ka-, Ku-, V- and Q-bands; applications go on public notice for **15 days** (down from 30), with a **30-day** completeness-check deadline on the Commission itself.

The milestone/bond structure splits by path: processing-round licensees keep the familiar **six-year 50% / nine-year 100%** deployment milestones, backed by a **$10 million surety bond** posted within 30 days of authorization that declines with deployment progress to **$0 at 90% deployment**; NGSO systems outside processing rounds instead follow **ITU-aligned milestones** (first satellite launched and maintained 90 days within 7 years; 10% of the constellation in 9 years, 50% in 12, 100% in 14). All space-station operators must file ephemeris data with a Space Bureau-designated space-situational-awareness (SSA) provider. Versus the pre-vote draft, the 7-day expedited public-notice path stayed dropped, and de-orbit language was softened from a mandate to a design-and-operate standard. Effective dates are delegated to the Space Bureau by public notice.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Adopted July 22, 2026: Part 25 → Part 100 (Report and Order + FNPRM); companion Upper C-band auction order covered in 2026-08-05_fcc-upper-cband-auction-rules-adopted.
- License terms: 20 years for most space/earth stations (shorter terms requestable).
- Processing rounds: voluntary, annual, band-specific; window Jan 1 – Oct 31; initial rounds for Ka-, Ku-, V-, Q-bands.
- Public notice 15 days; 30-day FCC completeness-check deadline.
- Processing-round milestones: 50% at 6 years / 100% at 9 years; $10M surety bond within 30 days of authorization, declining to $0 at 90% deployment.
- Non-processing-round NGSO milestones (ITU-aligned): 1 satellite launched + operated 90 days within 7 years; 10% at 9y; 50% at 12y; 100% at 14y.
- Mandatory ephemeris filing with a Space Bureau-designated SSA provider.
- Changes vs draft: 7-day expedited notice rejected; de-orbit obligation softened to "designed and operated to" standard; effective date via Bureau public notice.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This closes the loop opened by the 07-11 pre-vote entry: the first wholesale rewrite of US space licensing in the mega-constellation era is now adopted law-in-waiting, not a proposal. The two-track milestone design is the structurally new piece — it creates a genuine regulatory choice for NGSO operators between the bonded, faster domestic track (6y/9y + declining $10M bond) and the slower, bond-free ITU track (7/9/12/14 years), which will shape how speculative constellations file. Every operator in the bin's recent entries (Amazon Leo's waiver conditions, AST's SCS authorizations, SpaceX's Gen2) will eventually be administered under these mechanics, and the SSA ephemeris mandate is a new compliance surface for all of them. Watch: the Bureau's effective-date public notice and which bands open the first Jan–Oct processing round window in 2027.
