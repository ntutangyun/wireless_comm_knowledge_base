## Summary (EN)

An April 2026 market analysis by Giorgio Zanella (Technotrend Market Research) in EE Times Asia, ingested here as a deliberate backfill: it is the first third-party quantitative breakdown of NearLink's adoption structure in this knowledge base. The analysis sizes 2026 NearLink shipments at **12+ million NearLink-enabled smartphones** — overwhelmingly inside Huawei's ecosystem — plus roughly **11 million units** across secondary categories (TWS earbuds, smartwatches, PC accessories). For 2027, it projects the growth narrative shifting to consumer IoT, with keyboards, mice, and gaming controllers becoming the fastest-growing segment.

The piece frames NearLink's dual architecture as SLE (low-power connectivity, emphasising multi-channel operation versus Bluetooth's approach) and SLP (ranging/positioning, conceptually aligned with UWB). In automotive digital-key use cases it sees NearLink facing direct competition from UWB and emerging Bluetooth Channel Sounding.

On internationalisation the verdict is sober: adoption remains heavily concentrated in China; SparkLink Japan, European forums, and MWC 2026 partnerships are "early" and "largely pre-commercial"; near-term growth stays tied to China-led supply chains. Through 2030 the analyst expects NearLink to remain complementary rather than disruptive to Bluetooth/Wi-Fi, strongest in tightly integrated ecosystems, with the risk of remaining regionally constrained absent broader standardisation.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's NearLink coverage so far is event-driven — the HDC 2026 open-source announcement, the OpenHarmony code drop, the ITU listing, the 100-million-chip milestone. This backfill adds the missing structural layer: *who is actually shipping NearLink and in what proportions*. The smartphone-heavy 12M/11M split quantifies how far the technology still is from the peripheral-led volume story that Bluetooth rode to ubiquity, and the analyst's "complementary through 2030, China-centric without standardisation" thesis is a useful counterweight to launch-driven optimism elsewhere in the bin. Note the analysis predates the July 15 OpenHarmony open-source landing — the open-sourcing is precisely the kind of standardisation-adjacent move the author flags as the unlock for broader adoption, which makes this a baseline worth re-checking against 2027 shipment data.
