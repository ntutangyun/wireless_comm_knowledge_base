## Summary (EN)

An X post circulating on 2026-08-26 (≈2.9 k views, 54 likes, 4 reposts) carried the specification list for Huawei's newly announced **FreeBuds 7**, and the wireless-relevant line is that the earbuds ship with **NearLink (星闪) E2.0 audio at up to 16 Mbps**, carrying 24-bit / 48 kHz lossless, at 4.3 g per bud with upgraded active noise cancellation. Chinese coverage from the same week corroborates the announcement and adds the detail that matters more than the spec sheet: the FreeBuds 7 passed **NearLink Alliance E2.0 certification** (certificate `iSLA012026003-0000008`, connection mode 星闪 E2.0) and is described as the **first semi-open / half-in-ear (半入耳) earbud to support NearLink audio**. The product was announced in August 2026 with retail launch stated for September 2026, and the design drops Huawei's long-running teardrop stem shape for a straight-stem form.

The KB has tracked NearLink audio since the FreeBuds Pro 5 (Nov 2025, StarLink E2.0 with the L2HC 5.0 codec, 96 kHz/24-bit at up to 4.6 Mbps on a peak 16 Mbps link) and its first third-party appearance in the Edifier Comfo Clip Q2 (E2.0 certified June 2026). The FreeBuds 7 is the next step in that adoption curve: NearLink audio moving out of the sealed in-ear flagship tier and into the mainstream, non-sealing form factor.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

NearLink's audio push has so far been demonstrated on hardware where a high-bitrate lossless link is easy to justify: sealed flagship in-ears with a dedicated Kirin audio chip. A semi-open bud is a harder case — without a seal there is no isolation, ambient noise sets the practical noise floor, and the perceptual return on 24-bit/48 kHz lossless narrows sharply. That Huawei is shipping E2.0 here anyway suggests the value being sold is the **link** rather than the codec: NearLink's higher raw throughput and its scheduled-access design buy headroom against interference and latency rather than audible bit-depth, and the mainstream form factor is where that robustness argument gets tested against Bluetooth on volume.

It is also a concrete instance of the certification machinery the KB logged earlier this month: a named ISLA E2.0 certificate number attached to a shipping consumer product, showing the NearLink Alliance's scheme now gating real launches rather than sitting on paper. Watch items for a follow-up entry: which codec (L2HC version) the FreeBuds 7 actually uses, whether the 16 Mbps figure is the same peak link rate quoted for the Pro 5, and how the device behaves in a Bluetooth-coexistence sense when paired to non-HarmonyOS hosts.

## Why it matters / what's new (ZH)

星闪的音频攻势此前只在容易自圆其说的硬件上展示：带专用麒麟音频芯片的密封旗舰入耳。半入耳是更难的场景——没有密封就没有隔音，环境噪声决定了实际本底噪声，24 bit/48 kHz 无损带来的听感收益急剧收窄。华为仍然在这个档位上搭载 E2.0，说明它真正想卖的是**链路**而非编解码：星闪更高的原始吞吐与调度式接入设计，换来的是抗干扰与时延的余量，而不是可听的位深提升；而主流形态正是这套稳健性说辞与蓝牙在出货量上正面对撞的地方。

这也是本月早些时候记录的认证体系的一个具体落地：一个带编号的 ISLA E2.0 证书挂在了一款即将上市的消费产品上，说明星闪联盟的认证方案已经在为真实上市把关，而不只是停留在纸面。后续可跟进的观察点：FreeBuds 7 实际使用的编解码（L2HC 版本）、16 Mbps 是否与 Pro 5 所标峰值链路速率同源，以及在与非鸿蒙主机配对时的蓝牙共存表现。
