## Summary (EN)

SatNews (8 October 2026) reports on a SpaceX presentation delivered on 6 October 2026 in which SpaceX called operators' refusal to share ephemeris data (precise orbital positions and planned maneuver schedules) the single greatest collision risk facing LEO constellations. SpaceX presented a map sorting commercial, civil and military LEO operators into those that share ephemeris telemetry with it and those that do not; the article gives no counts or percentages.

According to the report, without updated ephemeris from both spacecraft, conjunction predictions can produce false warnings or miss real risks, and an unannounced maneuver can put a satellite into the path of a previously safe one. The article says the lack of ephemeris exchange creates operational friction across the congested 400-600 km altitudes. Operators relying on public catalogs from the U.S. Space Force 18th Space Defense Squadron are said to face tracking latencies of several hours to days, and radar-based data has larger error ellipsoids than onboard GPS ephemeris, which can force unnecessary avoidance burns.

SpaceX says it has raised ephemeris sharing repeatedly in semiannual constellation status reports to the FCC with minimal progress, and urged the FCC, the FAA and international regulators to make orbital licensing conditional on mandatory open ephemeris exchange and automated conjunction coordination.
