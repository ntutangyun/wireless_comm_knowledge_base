## Summary (EN)

"From 5G to NearLink: Europe's next digital dependency risk" is a Comment by Jeroen Groenewegen-Lau (Lead Analyst, Mercator Institute for China Studies, MERICS, Berlin), dated 14 August 2026; the article states it was first published by The Wire China on 9 August 2026. The author argues that Europe is drifting into a technology dependence less visible than the one with Chinese 5G vendors, and that NearLink (星闪, also SparkLink), presented as China's alternative to Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, is entering Europe through imported smartphones, fitness trackers, digital keys and industrial control modules. He warns of lock-in effects before regulators act.

For background the piece says Huawei began developing NearLink after being expelled in 2019 from the Wi-Fi Alliance and the Bluetooth Special Interest Group following US sanctions. It cites the Mate 60 series in August 2023 as the first commercial deployment, NearLink's addition to HarmonyOS 5 in November 2024, and iSLA's 2025 report of over 100 million devices shipped, more than 1,200 members, 600 products and over 300 chip and module development boards. It says iSLA registered internationally in 2023, launched a Japanese offshoot in August 2025 and started the NearLink Europe Partner Program in March 2026, both targeting the automotive sector, and that Huawei is pursuing recognition as a global standard including through the ITU. The author contrasts NearLink, promoted as coexisting with Bluetooth and Wi-Fi and opening parts of its stack to developers, with the WAPI and NewIP campaigns.

For policy the author writes that the EU has most of the tools needed. He points to the Digital Networks Act and the Cyber Resilience Act (software bills of materials and lifecycle cybersecurity plans) and recommends that the Commission uphold its proposed Cybersecurity Act amendment, which would let Brussels classify embedded short-range connectivity as part of the security chain in critical sectors, designate NearLink-capable modules as sensitive components and require disclosure of embedded software stacks. He adds a coordinated EU position in global standard-setting bodies, and cautions that Europe should not wait for another "Huawei moment".

## Summary (ZH)

《From 5G to NearLink: Europe's next digital dependency risk》是德国墨卡托中国研究中心（MERICS，柏林）首席分析师 Jeroen Groenewegen-Lau 撰写的评论，日期为2026年8月14日；文章称其最初于2026年8月9日由 The Wire China 发表。作者认为欧洲正滑入一种比中国5G供应商更不显眼的技术依赖，并指出被描述为中国版蓝牙与 Wi-Fi 替代方案的星闪（NearLink，亦称 SparkLink）正通过进口智能手机、健身手环、数字钥匙和工业控制模块进入欧洲，警告在监管介入之前可能形成锁定效应。

背景方面，文章称华为在2019年因美国制裁被 Wi-Fi 联盟和蓝牙技术联盟除名后开始研发星闪；引用2023年8月 Mate 60 系列作为首次商用部署，2024年11月星闪加入 HarmonyOS 5，以及 iSLA 在2025年报告的出货超过1亿台设备、会员超过1,200家、产品600款、芯片与模组开发板300余款。文章称 iSLA 于2023年完成国际注册，2025年8月成立日本分支，2026年3月启动星闪欧洲伙伴计划，二者均面向汽车行业，并称华为正寻求成为全球标准，包括通过 ITU。作者将被宣传为与蓝牙和 Wi-Fi 共存、并向开发者开放部分协议栈的星闪，与 WAPI 和 NewIP 的做法作了对比。

政策方面，作者认为欧盟已具备大部分所需工具，并指出《数字网络法案》和《网络韧性法案》（软件物料清单和全生命周期网络安全计划），建议欧盟委员会坚持其提出的《网络安全法》修正案：该修正案将允许布鲁塞尔把嵌入式短距连接归入关键行业的安全链、将支持星闪的模块认定为敏感部件，并要求披露嵌入式软件栈。他还建议在全球标准制定机构中形成协调一致的欧盟立场，并提醒欧洲不应等到再次出现“华为时刻”。
