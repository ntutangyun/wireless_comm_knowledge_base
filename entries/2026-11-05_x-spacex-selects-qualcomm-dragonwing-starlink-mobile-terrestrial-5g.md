## Summary (EN)

On 2026-11-04 Qualcomm announced that SpaceX has selected its Dragonwing cellular infrastructure platforms for Starlink Mobile's planned nationwide terrestrial 5G network in the United States. Qualcomm's release describes a multi-year agreement covering tens of thousands of sites and gives SpaceX's stated plan to begin the US rollout in 2027. According to Qualcomm as relayed by Axios, Qualcomm will provide the chips and foundational software for radio and baseband processing, while SpaceX will develop additional software and other network elements. Qualcomm's own press release names the platforms as Dragonwing QRU100 (radio unit) and QDU100 (distributed unit) and says the network will use Massive MIMO radio units with 64 transmit and 64 receive paths (64T64R). Qualcomm executive vice president Durga Malladi declined to disclose financial terms or contractual guarantees. Axios quotes Mobile Experts analyst Joe Madden reading the deal as a commitment to traditional high-power cellular infrastructure rather than relying mainly on small cells attached to Starlink broadband terminals; he also estimates total spend on the combined satellite and terrestrial network at no less than 100 billion dollars including spectrum (an analyst estimate, not a company figure). The Axios article could not be re-read for verification, so the Axios-attributed points (Malladi's non-disclosure, Madden's reading and the 100-billion-dollar estimate) are reported as attributed.

This entry comes from the social sweep: the announcement was spread widely on X within hours (Qualcomm's own post, analyst commentary, a post reporting that the design brings 64T64R Massive MIMO, normally run at 3.5 GHz and above, to SpaceX's 2 GHz band, per a third-party summary), and a Hacker News submission linked the Qualcomm press release.
