## Summary (EN)

Light Reading (Michelle Donegan, 9 November 2026) reports remarks by Verizon CTO Yago Tenorio at the Brooklyn AI-Native 6G Summit about the carrier's 7GHz field test. The article says Verizon and Ericsson made what it calls the first 6G call in the field outside in the 7GHz band in October using pre-commercial equipment (the KB covers the Ericsson press release for that call in 2026-10-23_verizon-ericsson-pre-standard-6g-field-call-7ghz-concord). The new material here is the follow-up assessment: Tenorio called the results "not super encouraging" for indoor coverage. Verizon built a small house with various wall and window materials to measure in-building signal losses, and the test showed 7GHz is not as good as midband spectrum for getting signals indoors.

Tenorio said "7GHz versus 3.5GHz" has penetration challenges that could be addressed with denser antennas, more active elements, higher output power and higher EIRP limits, and that 7GHz will likely still struggle to match the footprint of C-band (roughly 3.7GHz to 4.2GHz). He identified access to channels up to 400MHz wide, which the article says 6G standards call for, as the main challenge: he described 2.7GHz as "narrow", 4GHz as "uncertain" and 7GHz as "challenged", with none offering a clear path to a 400MHz channel.

The article also relays US policy context: the NTIA is studying four bands (1675-1695MHz, 2700-2900MHz, 4400-4940MHz and 7125-7400MHz) and will publish its 7GHz report at a 10 December event, covering how much spectrum can be cleared, when it can be accessed and the cost of relocating federal users. NTIA chief Arielle Roth is quoted as hoping the FCC will quickly open a rulemaking for a 7GHz auction, and the US plans to lobby for 7GHz at next year's ITU World Radiocommunication Conference.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Test: outdoor 7GHz field call in October (pre-commercial equipment, per the article) followed by an indoor-loss measurement in a purpose-built house with varied wall and window materials.
- Verdict quoted from the CTO: indoor results "not super encouraging"; mitigation options named are denser antennas, more active elements, higher output power and higher EIRP limits.
- Bandwidth point: 6G standards call for channels up to 400MHz; 2.7GHz is "narrow", 4GHz "uncertain", 7GHz "challenged" in the CTO's words.
- Sensing: the article says wide channels matter for integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), as shown by two Verizon trials with Nokia and Qualcomm.
- Other items in the article: the FCC granted Verizon a temporary licence to test 6G use cases in 4GHz spectrum around its Los Angeles lab; the carrier added nine members to its 6G Innovation Forum; the CTO named AI wearables, ISAC and symmetrical uplink as becoming clear use cases.
- Dates: NTIA 7GHz report due at a 10 December 2026 event (as stated in the article); the article's own date is 9 November 2026.
