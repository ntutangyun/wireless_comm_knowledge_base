## Summary (EN)

Russia's answer to Starlink — the Rassvet ("Dawn") LEO broadband constellation built by Bureau 1440 — is visibly struggling with orbit raising. Of the second batch of 16 satellites launched on 2026-07-19/20, none climbed above ~360 km: two failed their orbit-raising maneuver entirely, descended below 300 km, and are expected to burn up on reentry; three more are stalled at 310–330 km; the remaining nine are maneuvering but sit roughly 200 km below where the first (test) batch was at the same mission stage. The first batch itself never reached the planned 800 km operational altitude — 14 satellites formed a chain slightly above 500 km, and one of the original 16 was confirmed lost in June. Analysis threads by the Critical Threats Project (2026-09-01) frame the program as unable to compensate for Ukraine-related Starlink restrictions unless launch cadence and satellite reliability improve dramatically. This entry reached the KB via a social-media echo (X live search) rather than any satellite-scout source — first Rassvet/Bureau 1440 entry in the KB.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Planned operational altitude: 800 km; best achieved so far: a 14-satellite chain slightly above 500 km (batch 1).
- Batch 2 (16 satellites, launched 2026-07-19/20): 2 satellites below 300 km and decaying; 3 stalled at 310–330 km; 9 maneuvering ~200 km below batch-1's altitude profile at the equivalent stage; none above ~360 km.
- Batch 1 (16 satellites, launched March 2026): 1 confirmed lost in June; the remainder never reached operational altitude.
- Failure mode is orbit raising (propulsion/operations), not launch: the Soyuz insertions themselves succeeded.
- Program owner: Bureau 1440 (rough analogue of SpaceX's vertically-integrated model); strategic driver is replacing Starlink connectivity Russia cannot access over Ukraine.
