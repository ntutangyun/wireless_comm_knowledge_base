## Summary (EN)

Spanish operator MasOrange announced it has completed trials with Ericsson of advanced antenna systems in the 6 GHz band, evolving today's 64-transmitter/receiver (64TR) massive-MIMO panels to a 256TR configuration — potentially quadrupling the number of radiating elements. The trial validated advanced beamforming that steers the transmission beam toward each mobile user's specific location, which MasOrange says improves spectrum utilisation and increases network capacity, and, per the Spanish-language coverage, greater energy efficiency. Coverage of the announcement (reported first in Spanish tech/telecom press, with an English write-up from TelecomTV on 2026-09-04) is explicit that this is equipment/spectrum validation, not a commercial rollout: the 6 GHz band is being positioned as the operator's analogue of the role 3.5 GHz played for 5G; per the Spanish-language coverage, this is in preparation for 6G infrastructure expected around 2030. This item reached the KB via a social-media echo (X live search, "6G" search term) rather than a cellular-scout primary source.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Antenna evolution: 64TR (transmit/receive elements) → 256TR, potentially quadrupling radiating elements versus current massive-MIMO panels.
- Beamforming: signal steering concentrated toward each user's real-time location — the stated mechanism for the capacity and efficiency gains.
- Claimed benefits: improved spectrum efficiency and increased network capacity; per the Spanish-language coverage, also reduced per-user energy consumption.
- Band significance: 6 GHz framed as playing the role for 6G that 3.5 GHz mid-band played for 5G — a candidate anchor band for next-gen massive-MIMO deployments.
- Status: technical/equipment validation only; no commercial timeline announced beyond the industry's general ~2030 6G target.
- Partners: MasOrange (Orange + MásMóvil joint venture, Spain) and Ericsson.
