## Summary (EN)

India's Digital Communications Commission (DCC) approved the majority of TRAI's recommendations on satellite-communications (satcom) spectrum at a meeting held 2026-09-03. The approval sets a revenue-based spectrum-fee framework for both geostationary (GSO) and non-geostationary (NGSO) fixed- and mobile-satellite operators, and directly affects the three companies already licensed to offer satcom services in India: Starlink (SpaceX), Eutelsat OneWeb (Bharti-backed), and Jio Satellite Communications (Reliance).

## Summary (ZH)

印度数字通信委员会（DCC）于 2026 年 9 月 3 日的会议上批准了 TRAI（印度电信监管局）关于卫星通信（satcom）频谱的绝大多数建议。此次批准为地球静止轨道（GSO）与非地球静止轨道（NGSO）的固定卫星业务与移动卫星业务运营商设定了基于营收的频谱费框架，直接影响印度境内已获牌照的三家运营商：Starlink（SpaceX 旗下）、Eutelsat OneWeb（Bharti 集团支持）与 Jio Satellite Communications（信实集团旗下）。

## Key technical points (EN)

- Spectrum fee: a percentage of Adjusted Gross Revenue. Reports of the approved rate differ — one account has TRAI's 4% recommendation with DoT favouring 5%; Business Standard (2026-09-08) reports the DCC approving 5% with a discount to 4% in rural and remote areas. The fee applies uniformly across GSO and NGSO fixed-satellite service (FSS) and mobile-satellite service (MSS) operators.
- Minimum charge floor: Rs. 3,500 per MHz annually, regardless of AGR-based calculation.
- Geographic differentiation: an additional Rs. 500 per subscriber per year for urban service; no additional per-subscriber levy for rural service, with proposed subsidies for user terminals in underserved areas, where the one-time hardware cost is typically Rs 20,000-50,000.
- Spectrum allocation term: five years, with a possible two-year extension.
- What is NOT yet resolved: commercial rollout still requires (1) the actual spectrum allocation process, (2) Cabinet-level approval of the final pricing/allocation mechanism, and (3) a second-stage security clearance from India's security agencies for each operator, plus ratification by an inter-ministerial panel and FDI clearance (per Business Standard) — so the DCC approval clears a regulatory-framework hurdle, not a launch-readiness one.

## Key technical points (ZH)

- 频谱使用费按调整后总收入（AGR）的百分比计。各方报道的核定费率不一：一种说法是 TRAI 建议 4%、电信部倾向 5%；《Business Standard》（2026-09-08）则报道 DCC 核定为 5%，农村与偏远地区优惠至 4%。该费用对 GSO 与 NGSO 的固定卫星业务（FSS）及移动卫星业务（MSS）运营商统一适用。
- 最低收费下限：无论按 AGR 计算结果如何，每 MHz 每年至少收取 3,500 卢比。
- 地域差异化：城市服务每用户每年额外加收 500 卢比；农村服务不加收此项费用，且拟对欠发达地区的用户终端提供补贴——该终端的一次性硬件成本通常为 2 万至 5 万卢比。
- 频谱分配期限：5 年，可延长 2 年。
- 尚未解决的事项：商用落地仍需（1）完成实际频谱分配流程，（2）内阁层面批准最终定价/分配机制，以及（3）安全机构对各运营商的第二阶段安全审查，此外还需跨部委小组批准与外资（FDI）审批（据《Business Standard》）——因此本次 DCC 批准只是跨过了监管框架层面的一道障碍，而非具备商用发牌条件。

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This is the concrete pricing/allocation mechanism that determines whether Starlink, OneWeb, and Jio Satellite can actually operate commercially in India — a market whose licensing debate (administrative vs. auction-based spectrum allocation for satcom) has run for over a year. The KB has not previously tracked this specific regulatory milestone; it is a distinct, dated event from the general satcom-in-India licensing storyline and carries the 4% AGR and Rs 3,500/MHz figures (first published in TRAI's May 2025 recommendations) forward to DCC-level approval (TRAI's recommendations alone remained non-binding until this step). It also illustrates a recurring NGSO-regulation pattern the KB tracks elsewhere: revenue-share spectrum pricing (rather than one-time auction) as the mechanism several jurisdictions are converging on for satellite broadband licensing.

## Why it matters / what's new (ZH)

这是决定 Starlink、OneWeb 与 Jio Satellite 能否在印度真正开展商业运营的具体定价/分配机制——印度卫星通信的发牌方式之争（行政审批式分配 vs. 拍卖式分配）已持续一年有余。知识库此前尚未跟踪过这一具体监管节点；它是一个独立的、有明确日期的事件，区别于笼统的"印度卫星通信发牌"叙事，并将 4% AGR 与每 MHz 3,500 卢比这两项数字（最早见于 TRAI 2025 年 5 月的建议）推进至 DCC 层面的批准（此前仅有 TRAI 建议，不具约束力）。这也印证了知识库在其他条目中观察到的一个 NGSO 监管趋势：多个司法辖区正趋同于采用基于营收分成的频谱定价机制（而非一次性拍卖）来规范卫星宽带发牌。
