## Summary (EN)

AST SpaceMobile formally notified the FCC on August 4, 2026 that it has initiated active direct-to-cellular (D2C) service operations in Japan with partner **Rakuten Mobile** — the company's **first commercial service activation outside the United States** (SatNews / Advanced Television, Aug 4). The service uses the BlueBird phased-array LEO constellation to connect **unmodified 4G/5G smartphones** over Rakuten's commercial cellular spectrum, routing through the terrestrial partner's core network, with coverage framed around Japan's main islands and mountainous remote regions. Rakuten is a founding strategic investor holding roughly 5.3% of AST (about 15.51 million shares), and Japan's government has allocated approximately $926 million toward national communication-resilience and satellite-infrastructure collaboration.

The activation lands the day before the scheduled August 5 Falcon 9 launch of BlueBirds 11–13 (the next Block 2 trio, FM6–FM8, combined mass ~13,500 kg) from Cape Canaveral — and notably ahead of AST's US commercial D2D timeline, which the company's July 15 8-K deferred to late-2026/early-2027 after the New Glenn ground-test anomaly forced a Falcon 9 pivot.

## Key technical points (EN)

- FCC notification filed August 4, 2026: active D2C operations in Japan — AST's first commercial activation outside the US.
- Partner: Rakuten Mobile (founding investor, ~5.3% equity / ~15.51M shares); services: voice, messaging, broadband data to unmodified 4G/5G handsets.
- Architecture: BlueBird LEO phased-array satellites on commercial cellular spectrum, traffic routed through the terrestrial partner core network.
- Japanese government funding: ~$926M allocated for communication resilience / satellite infrastructure collaboration.
- Context: BlueBirds 11–13 (Block 2, FM6–FM8, ~13,500 kg combined) scheduled on Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral Aug 5, 3:42 a.m. EDT; Block 2 designed for ~10x Block 1 bandwidth, 40 MHz beams, up to 120 Mbps peak (Block 1 measured peak: 98.9 Mbps).
- No measured Japan-service throughput/latency figures disclosed at activation.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The sat-direct-device bin has tracked AST through hardware milestones (Block 2 launch, 2026-06-18) and schedule setbacks (US commercial D2D deferred to early 2027, 2026-07-22) — this is the company's first actual commercial service activation anywhere, and it happened in Japan, not the US. The sequencing inverts the assumed rollout order: a partner-market activation (backed by sovereign resilience funding and an equity-holding MNO) beat the home market. It also gives the D2C race a third live-service geography after Starlink's US/22-country footprint and the Fastweb/Vodafone Italy trial. Watch next: confirmed deployment of BlueBirds 11–13 (launch window was Aug 5 — landing-confirmation pending) and first measured Japan throughput figures.
