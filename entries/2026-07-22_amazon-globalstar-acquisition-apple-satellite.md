## Summary (EN)

*(Deliberate ~3-month backfill: the KB's Amazon Leo thread — 300-satellite and 396-satellite milestones — predated or missed this structural event, which reshapes the direct-to-device landscape the thread tracks.)* On April 14, 2026, Amazon and Globalstar announced a definitive merger agreement under which **Amazon acquires Globalstar for $11.57 billion**, folding the veteran MSS operator — its LEO constellation, its licensed L-/S-band MSS spectrum, and its ground-segment — into the Amazon Leo satellite business. Regulatory approval is expected to complete in 2027. In a linked May 27 transaction, Amazon also takes over **Apple's stake in Globalstar**, and Amazon and Apple announced that **Amazon Leo will power satellite services for iPhone and Apple Watch — including Emergency SOS via satellite — starting in 2028**, succeeding the Globalstar-operated service that has backed iPhone 14-and-newer satellite features.

Strategically, the deal converts Amazon Leo from a broadband-only constellation into a broadband + direct-to-device operator with licensed MSS spectrum — the asset class that has driven every serious D2D play (SpaceX/EchoStar spectrum purchases, AST's carrier partnerships). Amazon has said its **next-generation D2D system will begin deploying in 2028**. The acquisition also lands against Amazon's FCC deployment-milestone pressure (roughly 1,600 satellites required by July 2026, extended with conditions — see the KB's 396-satellite entry).

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This completes the missing piece in the KB's D2D-consolidation picture: the three US D2D tracks are now SpaceX/Starlink (own spectrum + T-Mobile), AST SpaceMobile (carrier spectrum partnerships — see 2026-07-22_ast-spacemobile-d2d-deferral-2027), and Amazon Leo (acquired Globalstar MSS spectrum + the Apple device base). Apple switching its satellite-service backend from independent Globalstar to Amazon in 2028 puts the world's most valuable device franchise inside a hyperscaler's constellation — relevant context for the FCC's parallel move to open unlicensed bands to satellite links (2026-07-22_fcc-unlicensed-direct-to-satellite-nprm).
