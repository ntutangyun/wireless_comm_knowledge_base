## Summary (EN)

The Edifier (漫步者) Comfo Clip Q2 open-fit ear-clip earbuds are the first NearLink product from a brand other than Huawei to appear in the International SparkLink Alliance (iSLA) certification database. Multiple Chinese outlets (Kuai Keji / mydrivers, PConline, Sina Tech, EET-China) reported on June 25, 2026 that the device passed the alliance's NearLink E2.0 (星闪音频, NearLink audio) test certification under certificate number SLA012026003-0000006 — the first alliance certificate issued to a third-party brand, and only the third product overall to pass the NearLink audio certification set after Huawei's FreeBuds Pro 5 and FreeClip 2 series. The device uses a HiSilicon reference ("公版") design; the reports state that this configuration supports NearLink's low-latency link, long connection range and Polar-code-based interference resistance, but does *not* support lossless (48 kHz/24-bit) audio transport or the 2.4 GHz + 5.8 GHz dual-band anti-interference mode found in Huawei's flagship earbuds — i.e. the entry-level E2.0 audio profile on a reference platform.

On August 13-14, 2026 Edifier formally presented the Comfo Clip Q2 (Sina Tech / China.com launch coverage): it is built on the Shanghai HiSilicon 谛听 (Diting) audio solution, "fully connected to the open-source HarmonyOS (OpenHarmony) operating system", with the HiSilicon chip's "hundred-microsecond-class" transport capability cited for real-time AI noise reduction/voice pickup, multi-language live translation and an on-device Q&A voice function, and native HarmonyOS integration through the Edifier Connect app. Price and shipping date were still "subject to Edifier's official notice". NearLink audio (E2.0) is currently supported on Huawei nova 15/16, Pura X Max/90/80 and Mate 80/X7 series phones; NearLink audio's physical layer offers up to 16 Mbps, and full-feature implementations carry 48 kHz/24-bit lossless streams (~2.3 Mbps, L2HC).

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This closes a watch item this KB has carried since the 2026-08-17 certification-scheme entry: the first non-Huawei brand in the iSLA product-certification database. Until now every NearLink-audio (E2.0) product was a Huawei in-house design, which made the "China-centric, Huawei-centric" critique in the April 2026 EE Times Asia analysis (in this KB) hard to rebut on the audio side. Edifier — one of China's largest independent audio brands — shipping a certified E2.0 device is the first evidence that the NearLink audio ecosystem is opening to third-party brands, and it arrives via the route the ecosystem was designed for: a HiSilicon reference platform (谛听) plus OpenHarmony integration, rather than a bespoke Huawei stack. Two nuances are worth recording. First, the certified feature tier is deliberately entry-level — no lossless transport, no 5.8 GHz dual-band — so "E2.0 certified" does not imply feature parity with Huawei's flagships; the capability-generation label in the certification database (E2.0) is coarser than the actual profile split. Second, the ~7-week gap between certification (June 25) and product unveiling (August 13-14) confirms the certification database as a leading indicator for third-party NearLink launches, which is how this run found it. Watch items: whether a second third-party audio brand follows, whether any third-party device reaches the lossless/dual-band tier, and whether the reference-design path shows up in the Xiaohong-AI-style OpenHarmony device family.
