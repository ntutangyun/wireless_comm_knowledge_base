## Summary (EN)

SatNews (3 November 2026) reports, citing reports from 2 November 2026 attributed to unnamed sources, that SpaceX has held preliminary talks with EchoStar about either acquiring EchoStar's Boost Mobile subsidiary or obtaining wholesale access to Boost's cellular network. Neither company has formally commented, and the article presents the story as unconfirmed.

Per the article, Boost has about 7.4 million mobile subscribers plus customer-care infrastructure and national retail distribution, and is primarily a hybrid MVNO that leases capacity from AT&T and T-Mobile. It quotes LightShed Partners analysts as saying change-of-control provisions in those agreements require carrier approval before capacity can transfer to an acquirer, so a purchase would not automatically bypass the major U.S. carriers (an analyst view, not a confirmed contract term).

The article frames this as part of SpaceX's plan for a consumer service it calls "Starlink Mobile", a hybrid satellite-terrestrial network that would make SpaceX a direct mobile carrier, with a timeline for full-scale service by 2028. It restates the earlier EchoStar spectrum transfer (AWS-4 and H-block licences, up to $8.5B cash and up to $8.5B in SpaceX stock, plus about $2B of interim debt interest) with a parallel commercial agreement giving Boost subscribers access to Starlink Direct-to-Cell, and mentions SpaceX's recent agreement for Grain Management's 800 MHz spectrum portfolio and talks to lease ground sites from tower operators. Market reaction cited: EchoStar shares up about 3%, AST SpaceMobile down about 11%. The article also notes earlier petitions by AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon urging regulators to keep satellite operators out of licensed mid-band spectrum.
