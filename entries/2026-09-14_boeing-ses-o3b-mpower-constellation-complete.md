## Summary (EN)
On 2026-09-13, a SpaceX rocket launched the final three satellites (F11, F12, F13) of SES's O3b mPOWER medium-Earth-orbit (MEO) constellation from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, completing what Boeing calls "the initial O3b mPOWER satellite constellation" — 13 satellites in total. (Spaceflight Now identifies the vehicle as a Falcon 9, booster B1080, flying from SLC-40; satnews says only "a SpaceX rocket".) Each satellite is built on Boeing's 702X bus carrying the O3b mPOWER software-defined payload, which satnews describes as able to dynamically route bandwidth, focus beam power, and adjust signal capacity in real time to match shifting geographic demand. The three newly launched satellites are undergoing post-launch orbit-raising, checkout, and initialization, with entry into commercial service targeted for mid-2027. The constellation targets bandwidth-intensive, multi-sector customers — cruise lines, commercial airlines, mobile network operators, cloud service providers, and government users — plus remote-region applications such as telehealth and distance education.

## Key technical points (EN)
- **Constellation completion:** 13 satellites total after F11/F12/F13 launched 2026-09-13 — Boeing's wording is the "initial" O3b mPOWER constellation. (The count of 10 previously on orbit is not stated in the cited satnews piece; it follows from the 13 total minus the three launched here.)
- **Platform:** Boeing 702X bus. satnews describes it as "a standardized dual-use baseline for national security applications"; Boeing's own release describes the 702X as "a standardized dual-use system optimized for quick-delivery, high-performance constellations in any orbit" (i.e., not O3b-specific — a reusable bus line).
- **Payload:** software-defined, enabling real-time dynamic bandwidth routing, beam-power focusing, and capacity adjustment to match shifting demand. (This knowledge base's commentary, not a contrast drawn by satnews: this differs from fixed-beam/fixed-capacity legacy GEO/MEO payloads that serve fixed coverage areas with fixed capacity.)
- **Orbit / launch:** MEO; launched by SpaceX from Cape Canaveral SFS (Falcon 9 / B1080 / SLC-40 per Spaceflight Now).
- **Timeline:** post-launch orbit-raising and initialization underway; commercial service entry for the final three satellites targeted mid-2027.
- **Unverified/undisclosed:** per-satellite steerable-beam count (a widely repeated but unconfirmed public figure of "5,000+ beams per satellite" could not be independently verified against Boeing's or SES's own materials as of this entry and is therefore omitted above) and specific per-beam throughput figures.
