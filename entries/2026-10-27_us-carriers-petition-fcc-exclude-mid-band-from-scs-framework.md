## Summary (EN)

SatNews (26 October 2026) reports that AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon, working jointly through the wireless trade association CTIA, have used filings and meetings with commissioners to ask the FCC to restrict its proposed Supplemental Coverage from Space (SCS) framework before the Commission's open meeting on Thursday 29 October 2026. The framework is the item described in the KB's 2026-10-23 entry on the FCC's tentative agenda, which would make 482 MHz available for space-based cellular services.

According to the article, the carriers want mid-band frequencies kept out of satellite operations and reserved for terrestrial build-outs, and ask that 290 MHz be removed from the 2.5 GHz and 3.45 GHz allocations, roughly 60% of the 482 MHz pool (290 of 482 MHz, the article's arithmetic). Their stated arguments are that satellite transmissions in mid-band would create serious interference risk for terrestrial 5G, and CTIA's warning that secondary orbital operations could degrade commercial mobile networks serving hundreds of millions of consumers. CTIA is also reported to urge stricter power flux density thresholds so that satellite downlinks do not overpower ground-based cell towers; no threshold values are given.

The article frames the Commission's options (the agenda lists the item as a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking) as keeping the 482 MHz allocation, adopting power limits, or removing the contested mid-band blocks. It places the dispute alongside an early-October joint venture by the three carriers to standardise satellite direct-to-device connectivity as an open-access neutral layer, and the FCC's approval, weeks earlier, of SpaceX's 15,000-satellite direct-to-device constellation. The article gives no docket number, individual filing dates, named commissioners or response from satellite operators. This account rests on the single SatNews article. The article cites no filings, and no other report of the 290 MHz figure has been identified. The vote outcome is not yet known.
