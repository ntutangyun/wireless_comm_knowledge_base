## Summary (EN)
Kymeta announced on 4 September 2026 a $20 million order from a U.S. Department of Defense branch (customer not disclosed, described as withheld for security reasons) for more than 100 **Osprey u8** flat-panel satcom terminals, bundled with multi-orbit connectivity services on Kymeta's **Kymeta Broadband (KBB)** network. The Osprey u8 is described as a flat-panel terminal in a ruggedized, low-SWaP (size, weight, and power) configuration that supports both **Geostationary Orbit (GEO)** and **Low Earth Orbit (LEO)** connectivity, and is capable of switching seamlessly between **transmission security (TRANSEC)** and non-TRANSEC networks. The release presents a PACE (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency) communications approach as what Kymeta's current solutions support, and "**AutoPACE**" as the step beyond it: the release says Kymeta is "now shifting the dial to AutoPACE", an intelligent communications platform that removes the need for manual switching between connectivity sources. The release states the terminals have already been delivered, packaged with KBB connectivity services; it names no delivery schedule or shipping location. With this order, Kymeta states it has delivered more than $30 million in terminals and connectivity services to this same DoD branch in 2026. No specific frequency bands or data rates were disclosed in the release.

This entry adds a ground-terminal angle: a named commercial multi-orbit flat-panel terminal (Osprey u8) already fielded at volume (100+ units, $30M+ cumulative to one DoD branch in 2026) that switches between GEO and LEO connectivity and between TRANSEC and non-TRANSEC networks on a single physical terminal, plus a named automated-switching platform (AutoPACE) presented as the step beyond manual PACE switching. It is a data point on how the GEO-to-LEO transition is being absorbed at the terminal level for defense users, distinct from the constellation-deployment and ground-optics stories already in the KB this week.
