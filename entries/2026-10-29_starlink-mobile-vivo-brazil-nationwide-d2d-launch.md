## Summary (EN)

On 2026-10-26 Starlink announced on X that "Starlink Mobile is now live in Brazil with @vivobr", stating that 50 million Vivo customers "can use apps and messaging in cellular dead zones — with 7M phones already connected", and that "this is the first nationwide service to cover the entire country" (the claim is Starlink's own wording). The post says a next-generation service, "Starlink Mobile V2", will follow. Brazilian press the same day (Tecnoblog, InfoMoney) reported the service under the name "Vivo Satélite": a direct-to-device (D2D) link from Starlink's low-Earth-orbit satellites to ordinary LTE/5G phones, usable only where there is no terrestrial coverage, outdoors with a clear view of the sky, and with data roaming enabled. Supported uses per Vivo's customer communication are text and audio messages (WhatsApp, iMessage on iOS, Google Messages on Android), in-app calls, Google Maps / Apple Maps and the Vivo app; video calls and general web browsing are blocked. The service is free for a launch period; Tecnoblog and InfoMoney give no end date; Teletime, citing Vivo, reports 12 months free for consumers and three months for business customers (M2M terminals excluded). Tecnoblog says the companies did not comment when asked and Vivo had not formally announced the service to the press. Tecnoblog describes it as the first service of this format in Brazil.

On the radio and regulatory side, Tecnoblog reports that the link uses band 7 (2,500/2,600 MHz, auctioned for 4G in 2012); InfoMoney gives the 2.5 GHz range at up to 100 W transmit power, under an ANATEL experimental environment created in June whose authorisation has been renewed to 2029-04-22 with commercial exploitation allowed under the sandbox rules; during the initial test cycle satellite use was secondary and had to stop immediately if it interfered with primary services. Separately, on 2026-10-09 Starlink posted that ANATEL had voted to approve "Starlink Mobile's full 2 GHz access with our global S-band priority" for a future high-speed mobile broadband service; the launch reported here runs on band 7, not on that 2 GHz allocation. (DCD Brasil reports the grant as 15+15 MHz of S-band.) This story reached the knowledge base through the Hacker News and X sweeps rather than the satellite scout's Starlink sources, which had no item for it.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Source claims (Starlink X post, 2026-10-26): live nationwide in Brazil with Vivo; 50 M customers eligible; 7 M phones already connected; "first nationwide service to cover the entire country" is Starlink's own claim; Starlink Mobile V2 announced as the next step.
- Service scope (Tecnoblog / InfoMoney, citing Vivo customer communications): messaging apps, in-app calls, maps and the operator app; no video calls or general browsing; outdoor use with sky visibility; data roaming must be on; works on LTE/5G phones with a current SIM/eSIM and OS, no extra app or contract.
- Eligibility (Tecnoblog): most Vivo consumer plans (Pós, Total, Controle above R$ 40, Lite, Pré with recharges of at least R$ 30); Vivo Easy excluded; the compatible-phone list was not yet published at launch (InfoMoney says Vivo keeps a list of approved phones in its service channels).
- Spectrum: band 7 (2.5/2.6 GHz, per Tecnoblog); up to 100 W and the ANATEL experimental environment renewed to 2029-04-22 with commercial exploitation allowed, per InfoMoney, which describes satellite use as secondary during the initial test cycle. The separate 2 GHz S-band approval (ANATEL vote, 2026-10-09 per Starlink) is for the future higher-rate service.
- Context: on 2026-10-19 VEON and Starlink said Starlink Mobile had connected 11 million people (cumulative, at least one connection) across Ukraine, Kazakhstan and Bangladesh; Brazilian press reports that on 23 October, three days earlier, Brisanet, Unifique and iez! signed an agreement with AST SpaceMobile for direct-to-phone service.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The technical detail worth keeping is the split between what is live now (messaging-class service on band 7 (2.5/2.6 GHz) under an experimental authorisation) and what the 2 GHz S-band approval is for (a later higher-rate service), which the Starlink post does not distinguish.
