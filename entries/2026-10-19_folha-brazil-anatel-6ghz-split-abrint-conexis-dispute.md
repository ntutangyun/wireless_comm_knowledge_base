## Summary (EN)

Folha de S.Paulo (C-Level section, datelined Brasilia, 13 October 2026) reports that Brazil's telecom regulator Anatel is weighing how the 6 GHz band (5,925 to 7,125 MHz) is divided between mobile networks and Wi-Fi, with small and regional internet providers and the large telephony operators on opposite sides. According to the article, an August 2026 decision split the band: the upper part (the remainder above 6,425 MHz, nearly 60 percent by the article's figure) goes to mobile networks, and the lower part (5,925 to 6,425 MHz) is kept for Wi-Fi routers. The article says Anatel reversed an earlier decision to reach this split.

The article lays out the timeline: a public consultation on the auction notice for the upper 6 GHz mobile band closes in October 2026, the auction is expected in 2028, and two further consultations on automated frequency coordination (AFC) systems for Wi-Fi are under way. It also notes that 6G is expected to use this band eventually.

The parties' positions, as reported. Abrint, the internet providers' association (president Breno Vale), wants a larger share of the band for routers and proposes sharing between mobile and fixed internet with AFC, software that detects radio signals in real time and avoids interference; it says members deliver up to 10 Gbps to homes and would struggle without more spectrum. Conexis, the telephony operators' association (president Marcos Ferrari), opposes sharing, argues that the band is designated for mobile and that the August split follows international standards, and suggests fixed wireless access (FWA) as an alternative for home broadband. Anatel councilor Alexandre Freire, rapporteur of the August decision, says tests so far show that sharing the same channel between Wi-Fi and mobile causes harmful interference under the conditions tested, but that the conclusion is not permanent: Anatel created an experimental regulatory environment, so AFC could be reconsidered if future tests show coexistence without harming network quality. Carlos Nazareth of the research institution Inatel says Anatel must weigh which users benefit most from splitting or dedicating the band.

## Summary (ZH)

Folha de S.Paulo（C-Level 栏目，2026 年 10 月 13 日，发自巴西利亚）报道，巴西电信监管机构 Anatel 正在权衡 6 GHz 频段（5,925 至 7,125 MHz）如何在移动网络与 Wi-Fi 之间划分，中小型地区互联网服务商与大型电话运营商立场对立。据文章，2026 年 8 月的决定对频段作了划分：上段（6,425 MHz 以上的其余部分，按文章所述接近 60%）划给移动网络，下段（5,925 至 6,425 MHz）保留给 Wi-Fi 路由器。文章称 Anatel 为达成此划分推翻了此前的决定。

各方立场（按报道）。互联网服务商协会 Abrint（主席 Breno Vale）希望为路由器争取更大份额，提议在移动与固定互联网之间以 AFC（实时检测无线信号并避免干扰的软件）共享频段；称其会员向家庭提供高达 10 Gbps 的接入，若无更多频谱将难以为继。电话运营商协会 Conexis（主席 Marcos Ferrari）反对共享，认为该频段是为移动指定的，8 月的划分符合国际标准，并建议以固定无线接入（FWA）作为家庭宽带的替代方案。8 月决定的报告人、Anatel 委员 Alexandre Freire 表示，目前的测试表明在所测条件下 Wi-Fi 与移动共用同一信道会造成有害干扰，但该结论并非永久：Anatel 设立了实验性监管环境，如果未来测试表明共存不损害网络质量，AFC 仍可重新考虑。研究机构 Inatel 的 Carlos Nazareth 表示，Anatel 必须权衡划分或专用对哪些用户更有利。

## Key technical points (EN)

- Split as reported: lower 6 GHz (5,925 to 6,425 MHz) kept for Wi-Fi; upper 6 GHz (the remainder above 6,425 MHz) assigned to mobile, per the August 2026 decision.
- Process: consultation on the upper-band mobile auction notice closes in October 2026; auction expected 2028; two AFC-for-Wi-Fi consultations running in parallel.
- Abrint position: share the band between mobile and fixed internet using AFC.
- Conexis position: no sharing; FWA offered as the alternative for home broadband.
- Anatel position: tests so far show harmful interference when Wi-Fi and mobile share a channel; an experimental regulatory environment leaves room to revisit AFC if later tests show coexistence.
