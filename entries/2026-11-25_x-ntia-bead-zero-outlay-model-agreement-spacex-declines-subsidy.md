## Summary (EN)

On 2026-11-23 the US National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) announced a "BEAD Model Zero Outlay Agreement" on its BroadbandUSA site. According to that page, the agreement is for use with a low-Earth-orbit (LEO) provider that agrees to meet BEAD service and testing requirements at its awarded locations but declines BEAD subsidies. The NTIA page names no company and gives no dollar amount; it says only that the arrangement could save taxpayers "hundreds of millions of dollars" and that some states have been slow to sign contracts with large national LEO providers.

Press reporting connects the agreement to SpaceX (Starlink). Broadband Breakfast (2026-11-23) reports that SpaceX is walking away from "more than $700 million" and puts the forgone grant funding at about $738 million; a follow-up Broadband Breakfast article (2026-11-24) attributes the roughly $738 million figure to a Connected Nation tally. Blandin on Broadband (2026-11-24) quotes Broadband Breakfast and Govly, which gives approximately $738 million. Press reports also say the arrangement is not yet fully executed: SpaceX is said to be ready to sign, and states may accept the model agreement or rebid the affected locations.

The NTIA page is the primary source for the agreement's terms; the SpaceX link, the dollar amounts and the reasons below come from press reporting.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Primary source (NTIA BroadbandUSA, announced 2026-11-23): https://broadbandusa.ntia.gov/node/17211. The agreement itself is linked from that page as a PDF ("BEAD Model Zero Outlay Agreement").
- Per the NTIA page, the provider under the agreement must: provide service at BEAD-required speeds; provide customer premises equipment to BEAD subscribers at no additional cost; comply with BEAD performance-testing requirements; offer a low-cost service option; and submit semi-annual reports on performance and subscription statistics at BEAD-serviceable locations.
- Per the NTIA page, eligible entities should use the agreement with LEO providers that decline BEAD subsidies, and states that do not proceed must identify another provider to serve the locations at a cost comparable to the cost approved in their original Final Proposal.
- Dollar figure (press-reported): "more than $700 million" and about $738 million per Broadband Breakfast, which attributes the $738 million figure to a Connected Nation tally; approximately $738 million per Govly (via Blandin on Broadband).
- Reason for the move (press attribution, not stated on the NTIA page): an NTIA official is quoted as attributing it to friction with state contracts, including financial reporting and default-penalty provisions; the model agreement is reported to remove those provisions.
- Status per press: not yet fully executed; states may accept the model agreement or rebid the locations to other providers. The sources do not list the affected locations or states.
- Secondary sources: https://blandinonbroadband.org/2026/11/24/spacex-declines-bead-winnings-prefers-to-serve-locations-at-no-cost/ (citing Broadband Breakfast and Govly); https://broadbandbreakfast.com/spacex-declines-bead-winnings-wants-to-serve-locations-at-no-cost/ ; https://broadbandbreakfast.com/fiber-isps-advocates-dont-like-spacexs-bead-deal/

## Key technical points (ZH)

- 状态（据媒体）：尚未完全签署；各州可接受示范协议或将相关地点重新招标给其他运营商。上述来源未列出具体地点和州。
