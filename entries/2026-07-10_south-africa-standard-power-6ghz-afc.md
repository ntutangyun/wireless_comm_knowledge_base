## Summary (EN)

South Africa's telecom regulator ICASA is moving to approve **standard-power operation in the lower 6 GHz band under AFC (Automatic Frequency Coordination)**, with the regulation expected to be signed into law by the end of 2026 (Wi-Fi NOW, July 9, 2026, Claus Hetting). Licensed WISPs will register radio and site details in an AFC database to receive channel allocations; the AFC protects incumbent satellite operations from interference.

The distinctive architectural choice is the AFC provider: the **CSIR (Council for Scientific and Industrial Research), a government body, runs the AFC based on OpenAFC** — the open-source AFC platform from the Telecom Infra Project — with a possible later transition to private-sector provisioning. Field trials are already underway at two sites in KwaZulu-Natal province. WAPA (Wireless Access Providers' Association) executive committee member Paul Colmer is quoted: deployment of standard-power 6 GHz connectivity can start "as soon as the regulation is signed into law."

The article frames the move as a leadership example in affordable connectivity — a developing-market regulator enabling ready-to-deploy 6 GHz Wi-Fi while Europe's RSPG recommends reserving the upper 6 GHz for future licensed mobile/6G use.

## Key technical points (EN)

- ICASA to approve **lower 6 GHz standard power with AFC**; expected written into law by end of 2026.
- AFC operated by **CSIR (government body) on TIP's open-source OpenAFC platform** — the first prominent government-run OpenAFC deployment reported; may later move to a private provider.
- Licensed WISPs register radios + site details in the AFC database to receive channel allocations; incumbents protected are satellite operations.
- Field trials live at **two sites in KwaZulu-Natal**.
- Deployment can begin immediately on signature (WAPA's Paul Colmer).

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's 6 GHz regulatory thread so far covers the US (2026-05-21_fcc-6ghz-rule-changes), the EU/RSPG split and the UK's shared approach (2026-05-04_eu-rspg-upper-6ghz-decision, 2026-05-28_wifinow-uk-eu-upper-6ghz-regulation). This is the **first African standard-power datapoint** — and the first reported case of a *government agency* running an **OpenAFC-based** coordination system, which lowers the entry cost for other developing-market regulators (no commercial AFC vendor required). If the end-2026 signature holds, South Africa would leapfrog most of Europe on usable 6 GHz Wi-Fi capacity for fixed-wireless and community networks.
