## Summary (EN)

A Ministry of Communications statement dated 22 July 2026 gives the current status of India's national 6G programme. The Bharat 6G Alliance (B6GA) — the industry-academia body set up by the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) to organise India's 6G effort — had reached 90 member organisations as of 15 July 2026, spanning telecom service providers, equipment manufacturers, startups, R&D institutions and academia. It operates through seven working groups covering 6G technologies, spectrum, applications and use cases and revenue streams, devices and components, green and sustainability aspects, and outreach.

The spectrum working group's report became, after stakeholder consultation, an official DoT publication: *Spectrum Roadmap for 6G services*, issued by the Wireless Planning and Coordination (WPC) Wing on 3 February 2026. It is structured around three horizons. **Short term (2025-2026)** identifies 300 MHz at 6425-6725 MHz, 100 MHz at 7025-7125 MHz and 3000 MHz at 37-40 GHz, with the mid-band items subject to restrictions protecting space services. **Medium term (2027-2030)** adds 67 MHz at 1427-1518 MHz, a further 300 MHz at 6725-7025 MHz and 1000 MHz at 42.5-43.5 GHz, and flags 4400-4800 MHz, 7125-8400 MHz (or portions) and 14.8-15.35 GHz as WRC-27 Agenda Item 1.7 study bands. **Long term (2031-2035)** identifies 5000 MHz at 66-71 GHz and lists five sub-terahertz bands for WRC-31 study: 102-109.5, 151.5-164, 167-174.8, 209-226 and 252-275 GHz.

On the research side, the statement records a three-year 6G terahertz testbed project approved under the Telecom Technology Development Fund at SAMEER Kolkata, in collaboration with IIT Madras, IIT Guwahati and IIT Patna, and commits to opening 6G testbeds to startups and MSMEs alongside R&D grants for indigenous chipsets and components. Internationally, India contributed "ubiquitous connectivity" as a usage scenario to the ITU IMT-2030 framework and pushed coverage, interoperability and sustainability as 6G capabilities.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The cellular-spectrum bin in this KB has been dominated by US and European regulators — FCC auctions, NTIA federal-band studies, the upper-6 GHz argument in Europe. India is the second-largest mobile market on the planet and it has now put a dated, banded, three-horizon 6G plan on paper, which makes it possible to compare national positions rather than infer them. Two comparisons stand out. First, India places the upper 6 GHz range (6425-7125 MHz) in its *short-term* bucket for 2025-2026 — the same range whose IMT-versus-Wi-Fi allocation is still contested in the US and Europe, so India is effectively resolving that fight in favour of licensed IMT on a faster clock than either. Second, India schedules 7125-8400 MHz as a WRC-27 study band, which lines up with the upper-mid-band ("FR3") work the KB tracks on the research side — the same range that spectrum-sharing research is already modelling at 7 GHz now has a national regulatory timetable attached to it.

The sub-terahertz list is the other durable artefact. Five named bands from 102 GHz to 275 GHz, explicitly parked for WRC-31 study, is a concrete statement of where a major national programme expects 6G's high band to end up — and it is a useful yardstick for reading terahertz demonstrations. The 252-275 GHz entry in particular brackets the 300 GHz systems that NICT and others are already prototyping, so the research is running roughly one WRC cycle ahead of the regulatory identification, which is the normal and expected ordering but rarely so clearly documented.

This is the first entry in the KB covering India's national 6G programme governance, from a source added to the scan rotation specifically to close that gap. Caveats: identification in a national roadmap is not allocation, let alone auction — the short-term band list has no assignment mechanism or date attached, and the mid-band items are hedged with space-service protection restrictions whose severity is not quantified in the summary. Watch for the WRC-27 preparatory positions India files on Agenda Item 1.7, for a DoT consultation or auction notice on the 6425-7125 MHz range, and for whether the TTDF terahertz testbed outputs feed India's ITU-R and 3GPP contributions.

## Why it matters / what's new (ZH)

本库 cellular-spectrum 栏目此前由美欧监管方主导——FCC 拍卖、NTIA 联邦频段研究、欧洲的上 6 GHz 之争。印度是全球第二大移动市场，如今把带日期、带频段、分三阶段的 6G 规划落在纸面，使得各国立场可以直接比较而非推断。有两处对比尤为突出。其一，印度把上 6 GHz（6425-7125 MHz）放入 2025-2026 的**短期**篮子——而该范围的"IMT 对 Wi-Fi"归属在美欧仍有争议，印度实际上以快于两者的节奏把这场争论裁向许可 IMT。其二，印度把 7125-8400 MHz 列为 WRC-27 研究频段，恰与本库在研究侧跟踪的上中频段（"FR3"）工作对齐——当下已有频谱共享研究在 7 GHz 建模的同一范围，现在有了国家级监管时间表。

亚太赫兹清单是另一个持久产物。从 102 GHz 到 275 GHz 的五个具名频段、明确留待 WRC-31 研究，是一个主要国家计划对"6G 高频段落点"的具体表态，也是解读太赫兹演示的有用标尺。尤其 252-275 GHz 一项，框住了 NICT 等已在原型化的 300 GHz 系统——即研究大致领先监管识别一个 WRC 周期，这本是常态与预期，但少有如此清晰的书面记录。

这是本库首个覆盖印度国家 6G 计划治理层的条目，来源正是为填补该缺口而加入扫描轮询的新源。保留意见：国家路线图中的"识别"不等于划分，更不等于拍卖——短期频段清单未附带指配机制或日期，中频段项目附有空间业务保护限制而其严格程度在摘要中未量化。关注印度就 WRC-27 议题 1.7 提交的筹备立场、DoT 就 6425-7125 MHz 的意见征询或拍卖公告，以及 TTDF 太赫兹测试床成果是否反哺印度的 ITU-R 与 3GPP 提案。
