## Summary (EN)

A Bluetooth SIG blog post by Jason Marcel (15 October 2026) presents Bluetooth as moving from an infotainment feature toward a "foundational connectivity platform" for connected vehicles. It cites ABI Research figures: an infotainment attach rate of 93% today, expected to reach 96% by 2030, and 73% growth for Bluetooth connectivity in key fobs between now and 2030; the post identifies key fobs and accessories as the biggest growth driver, while infotainment remains the largest application today.

The post says electrification, shared mobility and software-defined vehicles are driving demand for over-the-air updates, stronger cybersecurity and shorter development cycles, and that the industry is moving from legacy CAN and LIN buses to Ethernet and IP-based zonal architectures. In that model Bluetooth acts as a wireless access layer connecting service tools, sensors and provisioning workflows to the vehicle's Ethernet network. It also notes the Car Connectivity Consortium is incorporating Bluetooth into digital key implementations, and names digital access, wireless diagnostics, Ethernet-based service architectures, V2X and LE Audio/Auracast as emerging opportunities. The post contains no specification version numbers or protocol-level parameters.
