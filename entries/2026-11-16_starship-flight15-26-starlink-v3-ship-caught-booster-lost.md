## Summary (EN)

New Space Economy (14 Nov 2026) reports that Starship Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase, Texas, on 13 November 2026 at about 4:47 p.m. CST with Booster 22 and Ship 42. All 26 Starlink V3 satellites aboard were released. SpaceX had targeted about 14 orbits over more than 22 hours at an initial altitude of about 275 km; the article's results table lists the orbital phase at about 22.5 hours, against roughly three hours on Flight 14. Ship 42 then returned to Starbase and was captured by the tower arms. The article describes this as the first successful tower catch of a Starship returning from orbit.

The booster was not recovered. Booster 22 aborted its tower approach, diverted offshore and was lost in the Gulf of Mexico after its flight termination system activated. SpaceX spokesperson Dan Huot said the guidance system "detected that the booster was outside the conditions required to proceed with a catch" and initiated the diversion as intended. The article also attributes to SpaceX's Kate Tice a statement that the booster's engines "had stopped firing too early during its return". The root cause was still under investigation.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Vehicles: Booster 22 (the first planned return and catch of a Super Heavy V3 booster, per the article) and Ship 42.
- Payload: 26 Starlink V3 satellites, all released. The article notes that release is a launch milestone and that checkout and entry into service are separate steps; it gives no satellite health or capacity data.
- Orbital phase: about 14 orbits planned, about 22.5 hours flown, initial altitude about 275 km.
- Recovery: ship caught by the tower arms; booster diverted by its guidance system and lost offshore. The planned double catch was not achieved, and rapid reuse of the recovered ship was not demonstrated.
- Open questions listed by the article: the booster's failure cause, Ship 42's condition and whether it can fly again, inter-flight work, and whether both stages can be recovered repeatably.
