## Summary (EN)

The alliance's own news feed (posted 2026-11-06) announces that the "SparkLink Short-Range and Embodied Intelligence Technical Requirements Blue Book" went live on the International SparkLink Alliance (iSLA) website on 6 November. The post says the blue book was compiled jointly by more than thirty core member organisations of the alliance's embodied-intelligence special interest group (SIG), focuses on the integration of SparkLink with embodied intelligence, sets out the industry's actual technical requirements, builds what it calls a complete full-stack technology and network architecture, and is meant to guide joint industry work.

The post states that the blue book defines three core SparkLink application scenarios: connectivity inside a single machine body, collaborative interaction among multiple devices, and device-edge-cloud collaborative scheduling. It describes these as a roadmap for deeper integration of SparkLink and embodied intelligence, for joint standard-building and for large-scale deployment.

In its framing, the post says embodied intelligence places stricter demands on short-range wireless links, valuing deterministic latency, very low jitter and built-in security rather than only reach and speed, and that SparkLink has moved from an auxiliary connection to the "nervous system" of embodied intelligence. It cites the demonstrations at the 2026 SparkLink ecosystem expo (smart robot factory, unmanned smart retail, embodied household service and robot teleoperation) as having verified engineering reliability for real-time control and multi-robot collaboration, and lists low latency, interference resistance, high concurrency, integrated communication-sensing-positioning and Mesh ad-hoc networking as the advantages. The post gives the download route as the iSLA website's industrial ecology section, industry reports column. The post does not reproduce the blue book's content.
