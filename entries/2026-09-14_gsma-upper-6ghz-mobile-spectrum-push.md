## Summary (EN)

Speaking from Kuala Lumpur on 2026-09-07, GSMA Director General Vivek Badrinath announced that the mobile industry considers itself "ready to put the upper 6 GHz spectrum to work" and is formally urging national regulators to include the full upper 6 GHz band (6.425-7.125 GHz) in their spectrum roadmaps for full-power macro-cell mobile use. The GSMA states that more than 60 organisations across operators, device makers, chipset vendors, network equipment providers, and other members of the mobile ecosystem have joined the unified call, and that countries representing over 80% of the world's population already support mobile use of the band, which was harmonised for IMT at WRC-23. The announcement frames the band as capable of delivering 200-400 MHz channel bandwidths — sized for what the GSMA calls the 6G era — and notes that first spectrum assignments have already been made, with further awards expected across Europe/Middle East/Africa, Asia-Pacific, and the Americas as demand develops. No specific country commitments or operator deployment timelines are named; this is an advocacy/coordination statement, not a technical or regulatory outcome.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Band: upper 6 GHz, 6.425-7.125 GHz (700 MHz total), harmonised for IMT (mobile) use at WRC-23 (2023).
- Ask: regulators should place the *full* band (not a subset) in forward-looking national spectrum roadmaps, and license it for full-power macro-cell deployment.
- Coalition size: 60+ member organisations across the mobile ecosystem (operators, device makers, chipset vendors, network equipment providers, and other members of the mobile ecosystem) explicitly named as backing the unified message.
- Population coverage of support: countries representing >80% of world population already support mobile use of the band.
- Capacity framing: the band's width supports 200-400 MHz channels, which GSMA frames as the channel-bandwidth class needed for the 6G era.
- Status: first spectrum assignments already completed in some markets; further awards anticipated across EMEA, Asia-Pacific, and the Americas.
- No specific country, operator, or auction/assignment timeline is named in the announcement itself.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This KB has extensively tracked the *Wi-Fi side* of the upper-6-GHz contest (EU RSPG, Ofcom, IEEE 802.11 WNG LBT-bypass charter, WiFiNOW UK/EU coverage, the WiFi community's DNA open letter) and, on the cellular side, a single concrete technical trial (the 2026-09-07 MasOrange/Ericsson 256TR massive-MIMO 6 GHz field trial in Spain). This entry adds the missing piece: the cellular industry's own coordinated regulatory-advocacy position, stated by its trade body rather than inferred from vendor deployment activity. It gives future entries a baseline GSMA data point (60+ member coalition, >80% population support, WRC-23 harmonisation) to measure subsequent country-by-country allocation decisions against, and sharpens the KB's ongoing "who gets upper 6 GHz — Wi-Fi unlicensed access or licensed mobile macro-cell" storyline with the cellular side's own explicit ask rather than a technical proxy for it.

本知识库此前已大量收录上 6 GHz 频段争夺战中 *Wi-Fi 一方* 的动态（欧盟 RSPG、英国 Ofcom、IEEE 802.11 WNG 关于 LBT 豁免的立项、WiFiNOW 对英国/欧盟的报道、Wi-Fi 社区的 DNA 联名公开信），而在蜂窝一方，此前仅有一项具体的技术试验条目（2026-09-07 西班牙 MasOrange/爱立信 256TR 大规模 MIMO 6 GHz 外场试验）。本条目补上了缺失的一块拼图：蜂窝行业通过其行业组织正式发出的、协调一致的监管倡导立场，而非仅从厂商部署活动中间接推断而来。这为后续条目提供了一个基准性的 GSMA 数据点（60+ 成员联盟、超 80% 人口支持度、WRC-23 协调结果），可用来衡量各国后续逐一做出的频谱分配决定，也让知识库中持续跟踪的"上 6 GHz 究竟归 Wi-Fi 免许可使用，还是归许可制蜂窝宏站使用"这条故事线，补上了蜂窝一方明确表态的一环，而不再只能依赖技术层面的间接信号。
