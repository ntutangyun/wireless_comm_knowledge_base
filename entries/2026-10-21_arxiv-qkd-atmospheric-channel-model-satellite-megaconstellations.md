## Summary (EN)

Gándara Gándara, Vecino-Peñas, Fernández-Vilas, Fernández-Veiga and Díaz-Redondo (arXiv 2610.27856, submitted 20 October 2026, cs.NI; 11 pages, 11 figures) present an open atmospheric channel model for ground-to-satellite quantum optical links.

The model accounts for how molecular and aerosol absorption vary with altitude, together with atmospheric turbulence and channel loss. The authors validate it against data from the Micius and Hispasat missions, then test its viability for a hypothetical Starlink-based quantum network by examining single-photon transmittance, power balance, and uplink and downlink channel capacity.

According to the abstract, the model predicts real data under simple atmospheric assumptions, and the authors offer design guidelines for future quantum satellite missions. The abstract gives no numeric results.

## Key technical points (EN)

- **Scope**: ground-to-satellite quantum optical (QKD) links, uplink and downlink.
- **Model inputs**: altitude-dependent molecular and aerosol absorption, atmospheric turbulence, channel loss.
- **Validation**: data from the Micius and Hispasat missions.
- **Use case**: a hypothetical Starlink-based quantum network, assessed through single-photon transmittance, power balance and channel capacity.
- **Output**: design guidelines for future quantum satellite missions; the model is described as open.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

Satellite QKD link budgets depend on the atmospheric path, and the paper provides an open, mission-validated channel model that is then extended to a megaconstellation scenario. Evidence is as stated in the abstract (validation against two missions plus a hypothetical Starlink case); no numbers are given there.
