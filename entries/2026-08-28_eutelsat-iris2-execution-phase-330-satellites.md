## Summary (EN)

On 7 August 2026 Eutelsat announced that the EU's IRIS² sovereign-connectivity programme has passed its **First Rendez-Vous** milestone and is moving "from programme definition to execution," with Eutelsat leading the LEO segment inside the SpaceRISE consortium. The announcement finally puts hard numbers on a constellation the KB has so far only tracked as a policy aspiration: the IRIS² LEO segment will comprise **264 dual Mil-Ka/Ku-band satellites plus 66 Mil-Ka-band-only satellites — 330 spacecraft** in an inclined orbital architecture.

The architecture description is the technically load-bearing part. IRIS² LEO is specified with **beamforming antennas and 5G NTN**, dynamically allocating capacity to where demand actually is, and **optical inter-satellite links** to carry traffic between satellites so the system depends less on ground infrastructure. That is a full generational break from OneWeb Gen-1, which is a bent-pipe design with no ISLs at all — and it means the European sovereign network is being built on the same architectural assumptions (onboard processing, dynamic beam allocation, optical mesh, 3GPP NTN alignment) as Starlink Gen2 and Amazon Leo rather than on a European variant of the previous generation.

The money and the schedule are equally concrete. Eutelsat commits **€2.23 billion to IRIS² shared infrastructure** and a further **€1.16 billion to commercial infrastructure** (the Ku payload and commercial ground segment), spread over **2027–2034**, plus roughly **€1 billion for 229 additional OneWeb satellites** to strengthen the existing constellation through 2034. Against that, Eutelsat projects **over €10 billion of revenue across 2032–2040**, and says the arrangement gives it access to **more than twice its current OneWeb capacity** while sharing fixed costs and R&D across the consortium. First Ka-only satellites launch in **2029**; the commercial Ku LEO constellation reaches full operation in **mid-2032**.

## Key technical points (EN)

- **Milestone:** IRIS² passed First Rendez-Vous; programme transitions from definition to execution (announced 7 August 2026). Eutelsat leads the LEO segment within the SpaceRISE consortium.
- **Constellation:** **330 LEO satellites** — 264 dual Mil-Ka/Ku-band + 66 Mil-Ka-band only — in an inclined orbital architecture.
- **Architecture:** beamforming antennas + **5G NTN**, with capacity dynamically allocated by demand; **optical inter-satellite links** for on-orbit routing and reduced ground dependence.
- **Eutelsat investment:** €2.23 B shared infrastructure + €1.16 B commercial infrastructure (Ku payload + commercial ground) over 2027–2034 = **€3.39 B**; plus ~€1 B for **229 additional OneWeb satellites**.
- **Return projection:** >€10 B revenue 2032–2040; access to **>2× current OneWeb capacity**; shared fixed costs and R&D across consortium members.
- **Schedule:** first Ka-only satellites launch **2029**; commercial Ku LEO constellation fully operational **mid-2032**; OneWeb constellation strengthened through **2034**.
- Dual-band split (Mil-Ka for government/defence users, Ku for commercial) is the structural mechanism that lets one constellation serve a sovereign-security mandate and a commercial business case simultaneously.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's European-LEO thread has so far consisted of the 2026-05-28 EU 2 GHz MSS three-way spectrum split and the 2026-07-03 Eutelsat/Airbus order for 340 next-generation OneWeb satellites, in which IRIS² appeared only as a compatibility target ("IRIS²-ready" payloads, 5G integration, digital channelizers). This entry converts IRIS² from a compatibility target into a specified constellation with a satellite count, a band plan, an architecture, a capital commitment and a launch schedule — and it revises the OneWeb replenishment number the KB carried from that July entry: the additional order is now stated as **229 satellites for ~€1 B**, alongside, not instead of, the 330-satellite IRIS² fleet.

Three things are worth flagging for the KB's ongoing constellation comparison. First, **optical ISLs are now in the European baseline** — the 2026-07-03 entry described OneWeb Gen-2 as adding digital channelizers and onboard processing but said nothing about an optical mesh; IRIS² makes ground-independence an explicit design goal, which is the same convergence the KB has tracked in Starlink Gen2, Amazon Leo (2026-08-04 D2D filing) and Telesat Lightspeed (2026-08-05, 225 satellites with 500 MHz of military Ka). Second, the **Mil-Ka / Ku dual-band split** is the clearest statement yet of how a sovereign programme intends to co-fund itself: 66 satellites are defence-only, 264 carry both, and the commercial Ku business pays for the shared bus. Telesat's August announcement used the identical structure (military Ka added to commercially-funded satellites) — that is now a pattern, not a coincidence. Third, the schedule is long: 2029 first launch, mid-2032 commercial operation, 2034 for the OneWeb bridge. Against Starlink's ~10,000 satellites already flying and Amazon Leo's 354+ in orbit as of August 2026, IRIS² is a 2030s-decade asset whose relevance depends entirely on the OneWeb fleet holding the line until then — which is exactly what the €1 B / 229-satellite replenishment is buying.

## Why it matters / what's new (ZH)

知识库的欧洲低轨脉络此前由 2026-05-28 的欧盟 2 GHz MSS 三方频谱划分与 2026-07-03 的 Eutelsat/空客 340 颗下一代 OneWeb 卫星订单构成，其中 IRIS² 仅作为兼容目标出现（"IRIS² 就绪"载荷、5G 集成、数字信道化器）。本条目把 IRIS² 从兼容目标变成了有卫星数量、频段规划、架构、资本承诺与发射进度的确定星座——同时修订了知识库自 7 月条目沿用的 OneWeb 补网数字：增购订单现明确为 **229 颗卫星、约 10 亿欧元**，与 330 颗的 IRIS² 星队并行而非替代。

对知识库持续进行的星座对比，有三点值得标记。其一，**光学星间链路已进入欧洲基线**——2026-07-03 条目描述 OneWeb 第二代增加了数字信道化器与星上处理，但完全未提光学网状组网；IRIS² 把"降低对地面依赖"作为显式设计目标，与知识库跟踪的 Starlink Gen2、Amazon Leo（2026-08-04 D2D 申报）与 Telesat Lightspeed（2026-08-05，225 颗卫星、500 MHz 军用 Ka）呈现同一收敛趋势。其二，**Mil-Ka / Ku 双频划分**是迄今对"主权计划如何自筹资金"最清晰的表述：66 颗仅供国防，264 颗兼具双频，商业 Ku 业务为共享平台买单。Telesat 8 月的公告采用了完全相同的结构（在商业出资的卫星上加装军用 Ka）——这已是一种范式而非巧合。其三，进度很长：2029 年首发、2032 年年中商业运行、2034 年 OneWeb 过渡到期。相比 Starlink 已在轨约 10,000 颗、Amazon Leo 截至 2026 年 8 月已在轨 354 颗以上，IRIS² 是一项 2030 年代的资产，其现实意义完全取决于 OneWeb 星队能否撑到那时——而这正是 10 亿欧元 / 229 颗补网订单所购买的东西。
