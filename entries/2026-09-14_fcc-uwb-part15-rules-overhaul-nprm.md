## Summary (EN)

On 9 September 2026 the US Federal Communications Commission circulated as a draft a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM), "Unleashing the Power of Unlicensed Ultra-Wideband Devices" (ET Docket No. 26-245), for tentative consideration at the FCC's 30 September 2026 Open Meeting. The document is a draft, not an adopted or released item: its caption still reads `Adopted: [ ]  Released: [ ]`. The FCC's own fact sheet frames it as the first comprehensive review of the unlicensed UWB rules under Part 15 Subpart F since they were first adopted in 2002 — a quarter-century gap during which UWB use cases (digital car keys, secure door-lock ranging, asset and sports tags, ground-penetrating radar, through-wall imaging, and now AI-enabled sensing) grew well beyond what the original rules anticipated. The proceeding formally consolidates two outstanding petitions for rulemaking: Robert Bosch LLC's petition (RM-11844) and a joint petition (RM-12014) filed by five organisations — the Alliance for Automotive Innovation, the Car Connectivity Consortium, the FiRa Consortium, Profibus & Profinet International / omlox, and the Ultra-Wideband Alliance — both seeking amendments to the Part 15 Subpart F UWB rules.

The draft NPRM's concrete proposals: (1) update the regulatory definition of a UWB transmitter to accommodate additional modulation types beyond the original impulse-radio assumption; (2) modernize UWB device measurement procedures; (3) create an entirely new UWB device category, with technical parameters open for comment, specifically to support factory automation, autonomous navigation, and AI-enabled sensing — use cases that do not cleanly fit any existing Part 15 UWB category; (4) eliminate the written frequency-coordination requirement currently imposed on UWB imaging systems; (5) seek comment on the long-standing prohibitions on UWB operation in toys, ships, aircraft, and satellites, "including opportunities to facilitate UWB-based drone operations"; (6) seek comment on updated rules for ground-penetrating radar (GPR) devices; (7) seek comment on revisiting technical requirements such as emission limits, duty-cycle restrictions, and antenna requirements; (8) seek comment on simplifying/consolidating redundant marketing, eligibility, and labeling rules; and (9) seek comment on the manual or remote-controlled switch requirements and the "Ten Second" rule (§III.C.4 of the draft). No calendar comment deadlines exist yet: the caption sets `Comment Date: [30 days from publication in the Federal Register]` and `Reply Comment Date: [60 days from publication in the Federal Register]`, and no Federal Register publication has occurred. The document is explicitly a draft ("not a final, adopted action") subject to change before or at the Open Meeting vote.

## Summary (ZH)

NPRM草案的具体提案包括：（1）更新UWB发射机的监管定义，以容纳原始脉冲无线电假设之外的更多调制方式；（2）现代化UWB设备测量程序；（3）新设一个UWB设备类别（具体技术参数征求意见），专门支持工厂自动化、自主导航和AI感知等现有Part 15 UWB类别均无法妥善覆盖的应用；（4）取消目前对UWB成像系统强制要求的书面频率协调义务；（5）就UWB在玩具、船舶、飞机及卫星上使用的长期禁令征求意见，其中包括"为基于UWB的无人机运行创造条件的可能性"；（6）就更新地质雷达（GPR）设备规则征求意见；（7）就重新审视发射限值、占空比限制及天线要求等技术指标征求意见；（8）就简化/合并重复的市场准入、资格及标签规则征求意见；（9）就手动或遥控开关要求及"十秒规则"（Ten Second rule，草案第 III.C.4 节）征求意见。目前尚无具体的意见征询日历日期：文首栏位写明 `Comment Date: [自在《联邦公报》公布之日起 30 天]`、`Reply Comment Date: [自在《联邦公报》公布之日起 60 天]`，而该文件尚未在《联邦公报》公布。该文件明确为"尚未最终通过的草案"，在公开会议表决前后均可能变动。

## Key technical points (EN)

- First comprehensive review of Part 15 Subpart F UWB rules since their 2002 origin — the NPRM's own characterisation of the intervening period is that "Subsequent orders made further modifications to the rules, but did not fundamentally alter the structure of part 15, subpart F nor the cautious approach the Commission had taken when permitting the first UWB operations." The NPRM also cites "the large portfolio of UWB waiver applications that we have fielded to date" as part of the reason for opening the proceeding.
- Consolidates two petitions for rulemaking: Bosch (RM-11844) and a five-organisation joint petition (RM-12014) from the Alliance for Automotive Innovation, the Car Connectivity Consortium, the FiRa Consortium, Profibus & Profinet International / omlox, and the Ultra-Wideband Alliance — automotive, consumer-device, industrial-automation and UWB-industry bodies filing together, not an automotive-only ask.
- Proposes a brand-new UWB device category (parameters TBD via comment) explicitly targeting factory automation, autonomous navigation, and AI-enabled sensing. (This knowledge base's reading: the current rules were written around communications/ranging rather than the radar-sensing and AI-inference use cases now shipping in 802.15.4ab-era silicon.)
- Would remove the written coordination requirement for UWB imaging systems (GPR, through-wall) — a compliance burden reduction for public-safety and construction users.
- Seeks comment on the blanket prohibitions on UWB aboard toys, ships, aircraft, and satellites, "including opportunities to facilitate UWB-based drone operations" (FCC fact sheet wording).
- Also seeks comment on the manual/remote-controlled switch requirements and the "Ten Second" rule.
- Timeline: draft circulated 9 Sep 2026; Open Meeting consideration 30 Sep 2026; comment and reply-comment dates are set as 30 and 60 days from Federal Register publication, which has not yet happened. Filed as a "permit-but-disclose" proceeding under ET Docket No. 26-245.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's UWB bin has, until now, tracked only the standards-body side of UWB governance (IEEE 802.15.4z/4ab, FiRa Consortium application-layer certification). This is the first FCC/spectrum-regulator entry in the UWB bin — a distinct governance layer that determines what UWB devices are legally allowed to transmit, at what power, and for what use cases, independent of what IEEE or FiRa standardize. It is directly relevant to the 802.15.4ab silicon wave already tracked here (ST64UWB, Ceva/LG, Calterah, imec): a new FCC device category for "AI-enabled sensing" would give 4ab's native-radar mode a clearer regulatory home than the 2002-era rules provide today, and removing GPR/imaging coordination paperwork lowers deployment friction for the radar-sensing use cases in the `uwb-radar` bin. The drone/aircraft/satellite reconsideration is also new ground — no prior KB entry (UWB or otherwise) has covered UWB's air/space operating restrictions. This is a draft at the proposal stage only (comment deadlines keyed to a Federal Register publication that has not yet occurred; consideration scheduled for the Sep 30 Open Meeting) — the actual adopted rule text, if and when it lands, would be a distinct future landing-confirmation entry per the KB's standing guard against re-treading announcement-stage news as if it were final.

## Why it matters / what's new (ZH)

此前，本知识库的UWB板块只追踪了标准化机构层面的UWB治理（IEEE 802.15.4z/4ab、FiRa联盟应用层认证）。这是UWB板块首条来自FCC/频谱监管机构的条目——这是一层独立于IEEE或FiRa标准化工作之外、决定UWB设备可合法发射什么、以何种功率、用于何种场景的治理层。它与本库已追踪的802.15.4ab芯片浪潮（ST64UWB、Ceva/LG、Calterah、imec）直接相关：FCC拟新设的"AI感知"设备类别，将为4ab原生雷达模式提供比2002年旧规则更清晰的监管定位；取消地质雷达/成像系统的协调文书要求，也降低了`uwb-radar`板块所涉雷达感知应用的部署门槛。无人机/飞机/卫星限制的重新审视同样是本库此前未覆盖的新领域——此前没有任何UWB（或其他技术）条目涉及UWB的空中/太空运行限制。需要注意，这仍是处于提案阶段的草案（意见征询期限以尚未发生的《联邦公报》公布为起点；审议安排在9月30日公开会议）——若最终规则文本落地，按照本库对"将预告性新闻当作既成事实重复收录"的防护准则，届时应作为一条独立的后续"落地确认"条目单独收录。
