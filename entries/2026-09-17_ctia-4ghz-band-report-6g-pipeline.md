## Summary (EN)

On 16 September 2026 CTIA, the trade association of the US wireless industry, published a report titled "Optimizing the 4 GHz Band for America's Economic and National Security". The report defines the 4 GHz band as **4.4-4.94 GHz** and describes it as "a cornerstone of the spectrum pipeline identified by the Administration and Congress, alongside Upper C-Band, 2.7 GHz, and 7 GHz". It asks policymakers to "repurpose as much 4 GHz spectrum as possible" for full-power licensed use, noting that "NTIA has suggested 400 megahertz", and says "decisive policy action this fall will be critical". This is the same 4400-4940 MHz federal band whose NTIA study plan was cleared on 31 July 2026 (covered in this KB on 2026-08-15); the report is the industry side's position paper on what that study should conclude and how fast.

CTIA's case rests on three arguments as the report presents them. First, ecosystem readiness: the band "is supported through 3GPP Band n79, which covers 4.4-5.0 GHz, and nearly 1,500 devices on the market are already capable of using it", and "dozens of countries have already identified 4 GHz for mobile use". Second, demand: wireless demand grew "20% in the last year alone"; "analysts project U.S. networks will need 820 megahertz of additional mid-band spectrum", and the report cites Accenture as warning that without it "nearly one-third of future demand could go unmet", putting "an estimated $1.4 trillion in economic growth at risk". Third, the geopolitical framing the report itself supplies: 4 GHz "gives the United States a practical path to lead internationally with an alternative to the China-backed 6G ecosystem in upper 6 GHz, a band the United States has entirely assigned to unlicensed use".

On the federal incumbents (defense, homeland security and public safety systems), the report argues for "more efficient spectrum management" rather than clearing: many systems "operate only in specific places, at specific times", several "use relatively narrow channels", and highly directional point-to-point fixed links "can be more efficiently packed". It states that "4 GHz link density is 8 times less than that in the 6 GHz band", and that drive tests in Chicago and Denver "found little consistent government activity across most of the band, with few persistent signals appearing only near the top of the band". The tools it lists are geographic coordination, repacking into a smaller portion of the band, retuning, relocation to comparable spectrum, or modernisation, funded through the Spectrum Relocation Fund, with the AWS-3 transition offered as precedent. The report's economic claim is that repurposing 400 MHz of mid-band spectrum "would generate more than $1 trillion in GDP growth, create more than 6 million jobs, and help connect roughly 1.1 million additional households to broadband". Its requested timeline: NTIA should "establish clear targets" of "spectrum identification by 2028 and an FCC auction soon after, no later than 2030", and the United States "should also support global 4 GHz identification ahead of WRC-27". A companion CTIA press release the same day adds that the report wants the NTIA band study launched "in the next 60 days".

## Summary (ZH)

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The 4.4 GHz band entered formal NTIA study on 31 July 2026 with a 60-day congressional review window; this report is the first industry-side document in this KB's coverage to put concrete asks on that study, and it does so with dates (identification by 2028, auction by 2030) and a quantity (400 MHz) rather than general support. It also makes the n79 device-ecosystem argument explicit, which matters for how a future band plan would be drawn: an n79-aligned allocation would reuse existing 3GPP band definitions and device filters rather than requiring a new band. The report's incumbent-efficiency evidence (link-density comparison, drive tests) is CTIA's own and will presumably be contested by the federal users it describes; the NTIA study itself is the body that will adjudicate. For the wider 6G spectrum picture, the report's explicit contrast between a US-led 4 GHz path and the upper 6 GHz band, which the US has assigned to unlicensed use, is a statement of where the US mobile industry intends to argue at WRC-27.

4.4 GHz 频段于 2026 年 7 月 31 日进入 NTIA 正式研究、并附带 60 天国会审查期；本报告是本知识库覆盖范围内第一份就该研究提出具体诉求的行业文件，而且给出的是日期（2028 年识别、2030 年拍卖）和数量（400 MHz），而非泛泛支持。报告还把 n79 设备生态的论据说得很明确，这对未来频段规划的画法有实际影响：与 n79 对齐的分配可以沿用现有 3GPP 频段定义和终端滤波器，而不需要新增频段。报告关于在用系统效率的证据（链路密度对比、路测）是 CTIA 自己的，可以预期会受到其所描述的联邦用户质疑；最终裁决者是 NTIA 的研究本身。就更大的 6G 频谱格局而言，报告把"美国主导的 4 GHz 路径"与美国已整体划为免许可的高 6 GHz 频段明确对立起来，这表明了美国移动行业打算在 WRC-27 上采取的立场。
