## Summary (EN)

MDA Space announced on August 4, 2026 a **US$474 million contract increase** from Telesat that expands the Lightspeed LEO program in two ways. First, it adds **27 MDA AURORA satellites** to the 198 already being manufactured at MDA's Montréal facility, taking the build order to **225** — and lifting Telesat's *fully funded* constellation from 156 to 225 satellites (+69, since previously ordered spares now count toward the funded constellation). Second, it funds **the addition of 500 MHz of military Ka-band** to Lightspeed satellites currently in production, replacing the same amount of commercial Ka-band spectrum on those spacecraft.

The military Ka-band swap is tied to Canada's **Enhanced Satellite Communications Project–Polar (ESCP-P)**, positioning Lightspeed as a backbone for Canadian Arctic military communications — "Reliable military communication in the Arctic is a necessity for protecting Canadian sovereignty," said MDA Space CEO Mike Greenley. The majority of the $474M lands in MDA's Q3 2026 backlog. No new delivery or service dates were disclosed; earlier reporting has SpaceX deploying the initial Lightspeed constellation over roughly a year starting mid-2026, with Lightspeed-powered connectivity commercially targeted from late 2027.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Contract increase: US$474M (majority added to MDA's Q3 2026 backlog); announced August 4, 2026.
- Build order: 198 → 225 MDA AURORA next-generation broadband satellites (Montréal production line).
- Fully funded constellation: 156 → 225 satellites (+69).
- Spectrum change: +500 MHz military Ka-band on in-production satellites, replacing equal commercial Ka-band capacity.
- Program driver: Canada's Enhanced Satellite Communications Project–Polar (ESCP-P) — Arctic military communications.
- Deployment context (prior reporting): SpaceX launches over ~1 year from mid-2026; Lightspeed-powered service targeted from Q4 2027.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

This is the KB's **first Telesat Lightspeed entry** — the leo-constellations bin has covered Starlink, OneWeb/Eutelsat, Amazon Leo, Jio and K-Starlink, but not the fourth Western LEO broadband program. The interesting signal is *what* funded the expansion: not consumer broadband economics but a sovereign-military anchor tenant (ESCP-P), echoing the sovereignty-driven constellation theme (IRIS², K-Starlink, Jio) from the buyer side rather than the builder side. The 500 MHz commercial-to-military Ka-band swap on in-production satellites is also a concrete example of payload spectrum reconfiguration late in a manufacturing program. Watch: first Lightspeed launch (SpaceX, from mid-2026) and ESCP-P milestones.
