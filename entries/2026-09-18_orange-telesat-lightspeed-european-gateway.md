## Summary (EN)

On 2026-09-17, telecommunications operator Orange and Telesat LEO ULC (a subsidiary of Telesat Corporation) announced the commissioning of the first European gateway for the Telesat Lightspeed LEO constellation, per SatNews. The gateway is installed at Orange's World Teleport Association Tier-4 certified facility in Bercenay-en-Othe, France, selected for its operational-security certifications, existing ground-segment infrastructure, and proximity to major European fiber backbones; Orange supplies a dedicated high-speed fiber link from the teleport to Telesat's planned Point of Presence in Paris. The announcement describes the gateway as commissioned; the article says it will serve as a primary European entry point for space-to-ground data transmission.

The gateway's tracking-antenna subsystems are supplied by Intellian Technologies and are engineered to support the initial 225-satellite deployment phase of the Lightspeed constellation, which routes data via optical inter-satellite links between spacecraft before handing off to a ground station. Per the article, this architecture keeps customer data in orbit across inter-satellite links until it reaches a designated landing station or private customer ground interface, reducing intermediate terrestrial transit hops. Orange International Networks EVP Jean-Louis Le Roux and Telesat CCO Glenn Katz are both quoted welcoming the partnership; Katz's quote frames the gateway as part of Telesat's "build-out of the Telesat Lightspeed terrestrial infrastructure in advance of our anticipated global in-service date of Q1 2028," with plans to build additional gateways and landing facilities across North America, Europe, and other markets.

## Key technical points (EN)

- **Location**: Orange's World Teleport Association Tier-4 certified teleport, Bercenay-en-Othe, France; linked via dedicated Orange fiber to a planned Telesat Point of Presence in Paris.
- **Hardware**: gateway tracking antennas supplied by Intellian Technologies.
- **Constellation scale supported**: engineered for the initial 225-satellite Lightspeed deployment phase.
- **Network architecture**: optical inter-satellite links route data in orbit between spacecraft before handoff to the ground station/landing point, reducing intermediate terrestrial transit hops.
- **Program timeline**: gateway build-out is described as advancing ahead of Telesat's anticipated global in-service date of Q1 2028; further gateways/landing facilities planned across North America, Europe, and other markets.
- **No disclosed figures**: article gives no specific frequency band, throughput, or capacity numbers for the gateway itself.
