## Summary (EN)
On August 19, 2026, NTIA announced that the plan to repurpose the **2.7 GHz band (2.69–2.9 GHz)** for commercial use has cleared a key statutory milestone: the **Spectrum Relocation Fund (SRF) Technical Panel** — chaired by NTIA with OMB and FCC representatives — approved the detailed relocation plans of the incumbent federal agencies (**NOAA** and the **FAA**, whose weather and air-surveillance radars occupy the band) and transmitted them, with cost estimates, to the Office of Management and Budget. OMB has notified Congress, triggering the statutory **60-day review** by the Commerce and Appropriations Committees; once that completes, the agencies can draw SRF funding and begin the technical and operational work needed for NTIA to evaluate the feasibility of identifying the band for commercial repurposing. NTIA pitches 2.7 GHz as prime mid-band real estate — neighborhood-scale coverage with indoor penetration plus room for large contiguous channels — and frames the milestone within the mandate to identify 500 MHz of federal spectrum for commercial use within five years.

This is the second of the four federal study bands (1.6 / 2.7 / 4.4 / 7 GHz) to reach a concrete relocation-plan milestone, following the 4.4 GHz study-plan clearance on July 31 (in KB 2026-08-15). Context: the public-facing tracker for this pipeline is **Spectrum.gov**, the portal NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth launched at the 2026 CTIA Summit (May 7, 2026) to centralize federal spectrum policy, 6G pipeline progress, space-economy items, and WRC-27 preparation under one site.

## Key technical points (EN)
- Band: 2.69–2.9 GHz ("2.7 GHz"), ~210 MHz of federal mid-band; incumbents are NOAA and FAA radar systems.
- Milestone: SRF Technical Panel (NTIA chair + OMB + FCC) approved detailed relocation plans → transmitted to OMB with cost estimates → OMB notified Congress → 60-day Commerce/Appropriations review.
- After review, NOAA/FAA access Spectrum Relocation Fund money to begin the relocation engineering that feeds NTIA's feasibility evaluation.
- Positioning: coverage + indoor penetration + large contiguous channels; part of the 500 MHz-in-5-years federal identification mandate and the four-band 6G pipeline (1.6 / 2.7 / 4.4 / 7 GHz, all now under simultaneous study).
- Sequencing vs KB: 4.4 GHz study plan cleared Jul-31 (KB 08-15); 2.7 GHz is now the furthest along on the relocation-fund track.
- Spectrum.gov (launched May 7, 2026 at CTIA Summit) is the consolidated public tracker: federal spectrum policy, 6G pipeline, space economy, WRC-27.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
The KB's US spectrum-pipeline thread (NTIA 4.4 GHz study clearance 08-15, FCC upper C-band auction rules 08-05, AWS-3 Auction 113 close 08-18) has so far tracked *study* and *auction* events; this is the first entry where a federal band crosses onto the **relocation-funding** track — the stage where incumbent agencies get actual money to move, historically the strongest predictor that a band really reaches auction (AWS-3 itself followed this SRF path a decade ago). The NOAA/FAA radar incumbency also makes 2.7 GHz the pipeline band with the hardest coexistence problem (weather-radar protection), which will shape technical debate as 6G mid-band plans firm up. Spectrum.gov is folded in as context (its May-7 launch predates this entry; logged here because the KB had no record of it and it is the canonical tracker for every subsequent pipeline milestone).
