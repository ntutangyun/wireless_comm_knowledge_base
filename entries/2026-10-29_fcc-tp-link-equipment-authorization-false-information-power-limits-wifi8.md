## Summary (EN)

Tom's Hardware (Brandon Hill, 2026-10-28), citing Bloomberg, reports that in letters dated 2026-10-15 the FCC's Office of Engineering and Technology alleged that TP-Link "included false or incorrect information in its applications" for equipment authorizations covering four individual router models, including high-end Archer models. According to the report, three of the four routers also showed higher-than-allowed power levels in FCC testing. The article places the letters in the context of TP-Link's continuing attempts to obtain permission to sell new routers, including Wi-Fi 8 products, in the U.S. while competitors such as Amazon, Asus and Netgear have received conditional approval to sell wireless products in the U.S.

TP-Link's statement, quoted in the article, says that "routine spot checks have been a standard FCC practice across the consumer electronics industry for many years, and individual product test results can occasionally vary", that the company "fully supports the FCC's Office of Engineering and Technology", and that it will "address any confirmed technical issues as swiftly as possible". The article also recalls TP-Link's earlier October announcement of what Tom's Hardware describes as a $1 billion joint venture with Key Tronic to manufacture Wi-Fi 8 routers in the U.S. (TP-Link's 19 October release describes a letter of intent for U.S. assembly), and notes that members of Congress and several state attorneys general have separately raised complaints about the company. The story reached this knowledge base through the X live search, where the Tom's Hardware post was being reshared on 10-28; the KB has no earlier record of these FCC letters.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Letters dated 2026-10-15 from the FCC Office of Engineering and Technology, per Bloomberg as relayed by Tom's Hardware; four router models, allegation of "false or incorrect information" in equipment-authorization applications.
- Three of the four routers reportedly exceeded allowed power levels (the article says "power usage levels" and does not specify whether this is transmit power). The article says FCC router testing also covers electromagnetic compatibility, RF emissions and national-security compliance; the specific models, bands and measured levels are not given.
- Market-access context: conditional approvals reported for Amazon, Asus and Netgear (the underlying Covered List restriction on foreign-produced routers dates from 23 March 2026; see 2026-08-03_fcc-covered-list-foreign-routers).
- TP-Link response: spot checks are routine, test results "can occasionally vary", commitment to resolve confirmed issues; the company has moved its U.S. headquarters to California and announced the Key Tronic manufacturing venture.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB already tracks TP-Link's Wi-Fi 8 market-access situation from the product side (Archer 8 launch, 09-02), the regulatory side (Asus conditional approval, 09-11) and the manufacturing side (Key Tronic LOI, 10-20). The measured details (which models, how far over the limit, which bands) are not public in this report, so the entry records the allegation and the response as reported, not as established facts.
