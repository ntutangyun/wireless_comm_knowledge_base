## Summary (EN)

The OCUDU Ecosystem Foundation case study titled "How OCUDU Enables Open Source ISAC Experimentation for 6G" (page dated (last updated) 2026-11-04) was promoted on X on 2026-11-05. The page says Allbesmart developed an open-source 5G Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) testbed that integrates an OCUDU-based gNB, an OpenAirInterface-based core and sensing node, and NVIDIA GPU-powered edge AI, to overcome the data-access limits of closed commercial 5G hardware. It gives direct access to low-level physical-layer measurements using real 5G NR waveforms, monitored through a web dashboard, and enables real-time detection and tracking of drones, vehicles and people. The foundation states that setup time falls from months to hours and that the reproducible, open architecture accelerates 6G algorithm validation and lays a foundation for commercial "Sensing-as-a-Service" over existing cellular infrastructure. A collaborator at Allbesmart acknowledged the post on X. The linked PDF with measurements was not read for this entry, so no detection ranges or accuracy figures are given.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Architecture per the page: OCUDU-based gNB, OpenAirInterface-based core network and sensing node, NVIDIA GPU-powered edge AI, web dashboard.
- Motivation: closed commercial 5G gear does not expose raw radio data; the testbed exposes low-level physical-layer measurements from real 5G NR waveforms.
- Sensing targets stated: drones, vehicles and people, detected and tracked in real time.
- Claimed benefit: setup from months to hours (the foundation's wording).
- Not given on the page read: detection range, accuracy, frequency band, hardware radio front end.
