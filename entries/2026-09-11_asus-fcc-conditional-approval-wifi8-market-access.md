## Summary (EN)

Tom's Hardware reported on 10 September 2026 that Asus has been granted a Conditional Approval covering its consumer and business networking portfolio, letting those products continue to receive US equipment authorizations despite the Covered-List entry that otherwise blocks foreign-produced consumer routers. The report lists the covered lines as the RT Series, ROG GT Series, ROG Strix GS Series, TUF Gaming Series, ExpertWiFi Series, ZenWiFi Mesh Series, 5G MiFi Series, wireless repeaters and wireless access points, and states the approval runs until 6 March 2028, which it describes as 18 months from the date of issuance. It quotes the FCC as finding that "each of the Conditional Approvals constitutes 'a specific determination' by [Department of War] that such devices do not pose risks to U.S. national security" — so the underlying security determination is made by the Department of War, not by the FCC itself, with the FCC maintaining the resulting list.

The article places Asus alongside Netgear and Amazon eero/Leo as holders of conditional approvals, and reports that TP-Link, the largest player in the US wireless router market, still has not obtained one. It attributes that to unresolved US government uncertainty about TP-Link's claims of no ties to the Chinese government or military, and notes the consequence for products this KB already tracks: TP-Link has not confirmed a US release date for the Wi-Fi 8 "Ultra" routers it announced. On the Asus side the article notes the ROG NeoCore Wi-Fi 8 concept router shown at CES this year and the ROG Rapture GT-BN98 Pro consumer Wi-Fi 8 router announced in June, for which Asus claims a 2x real-world throughput uplift over its current Wi-Fi 7 products.

The grant is documented in the FCC's own record, not only in press coverage. Public Notice DA 26-957, released 9 September 2026 (WC Docket No. 18-89, ET Docket No. 21-232, EA Docket No. 21-233), lists "ASUS Computer International's RT Router Series, ROG GT Series, ROG Strix GS Series, TUF Gaming Series, ExpertWiFi Series, ZenWiFi Mesh Series, 5G MiFi Series, repeaters, and wireless access points (terminating March 6, 2028)", and the same rows appear on the FCC's published Covered List under Routers Conditional Approvals, dated 9 September 2026 – 6 March 2028. That list also carries Netgear (14 April 2026 – 1 October 2027) and eero LLC including Amazon Leo routers (22 April 2026 – 31 October 2027), matching the article's account of who already held approvals.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Primary source: FCC Public Notice DA 26-957, released 9 September 2026 (WC Docket No. 18-89, ET Docket No. 21-232, EA Docket No. 21-233), which records the Asus Conditional Approval as terminating 6 March 2028.
- Scope of the Asus grant as recorded: RT Series, ROG GT Series, ROG Strix GS Series, TUF Gaming Series, ExpertWiFi Series, ZenWiFi Mesh Series, 5G MiFi Series, wireless repeaters, wireless access points.
- Validity: 9 September 2026 to 6 March 2028, which the press coverage describes as 18 months from the date of issuance.
- Decision authority: the FCC is quoted describing each Conditional Approval as "a specific determination" by the Department of War that the devices do not pose risks to US national security. The FCC maintains the Covered List and the associated conditional-approval list.
- Covered-List wording for routers, as published by the FCC: "Routers produced in a foreign country, except routers which have been granted a Conditional Approval by DoW or DHS."
- Conditional approvals already published on the FCC list (9 September 2026 snapshot) include Netgear (14 Apr 2026 – 1 Oct 2027), Adtran SDG-class routers (14 Apr 2026 – 1 Oct 2027), eero LLC incl. Amazon Leo routers (22 Apr 2026 – 31 Oct 2027), Calix, Nokia, Alpha Networks, Sagemcom, Arcadyan, Hitron, Vantiva, Gemtek, Gryphon and Sercomm entries, with the Asus rows added to the same list on 9 September 2026.
- Most published router approvals carry a start date and an end date roughly 16 to 18 months later, matching the duration reported for Asus. Two supplemental rows inherit an earlier end date and so run about 16 months (Calix 24 July 2026 – 22 November 2027; Sagemcom 24 July 2026 – 5 December 2027).
- TP-Link, per the article, holds no such approval and has not confirmed a US release date for its announced Wi-Fi 8 "Ultra" routers.
- Separately, FCC Public Notice DA 26-286 (released 23 March 2026, ET Docket No. 21-232) waives the prohibitions in 47 CFR 2.932(b) and 2.1043(b) on Class I permissive changes for routers authorized before the 23 March 2026 Covered-List addition, so those already-authorized routers can keep receiving software and firmware updates. DA 26-454 (released 8 May 2026, same docket) then extended that waiver "at least until January 1, 2029" and widened it to cover analogous Class II permissive changes, superseding the 1 March 2027 date the original notice set.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's existing entry on the 23 March 2026 Covered-List action recorded the rule and noted that Netgear held conditional approvals while TP-Link did not. This entry records where that gate now stands for two specific vendors, and it matters because the gate sits directly upstream of Wi-Fi 8 product availability in the US rather than upstream of the technology itself. Both companies have announced consumer Wi-Fi 8 hardware — Asus the ROG Rapture GT-BN98 Pro, TP-Link the Archer "Ultra" line already covered here — and on the article's account only one of them currently has a path to US authorization for it.

The mechanism is also worth recording precisely, because the shorthand "FCC approval" used in press coverage is misleading about who decides. The FCC's own Covered-List text makes the exception turn on a Conditional Approval granted by DoW or DHS, and the FCC is quoted treating each approval as a specific determination by the Department of War. The published list also shows the approvals are time-boxed at roughly 18 months rather than permanent, so every holder faces a renewal point — Netgear's and Adtran's in October 2027, eero's and Nokia's in October 2027, and on the article's figure Asus's in March 2028. That makes this a recurring gate rather than a one-time clearance, which is the part most relevant to product roadmaps tracked in this KB.

Every element of this entry is anchored in the FCC's own record: the Asus rows and their 6 March 2028 termination date in Public Notice DA 26-957 and on the published Covered List, the router Covered-List wording, the DoW conditional-approval mechanism, and the Netgear, Adtran and eero approvals with their dates. The press coverage is what surfaced the grant; it is not what establishes it.

## Why it matters / what's new (ZH)

本知识库已有条目记录了 2026 年 3 月 23 日的受管制清单行动，并指出 Netgear 持有附条件批准而 TP-Link 没有。本条目记录该关卡在两家具体厂商身上的最新状态；其意义在于，这道关卡直接位于 Wi-Fi 8 产品在美国可得性的上游，而非位于技术本身的上游。两家公司都已发布消费级 Wi-Fi 8 硬件——华硕的 ROG Rapture GT-BN98 Pro，以及本库已收录的 TP-Link Archer「Ultra」产品线——而按该文章的说法，目前只有其中一家拥有在美国取得认证的通道。

该机制本身也值得准确记录，因为媒体常用的「FCC 批准」这一简称会让人误解决定权归属。FCC 官方的受管制清单文本明确规定，例外取决于 DoW 或 DHS 授予的附条件批准；FCC 亦被引述称每项批准都是战争部作出的特定认定。已公布的清单还显示这些批准是约 18 个月的限期授权而非永久许可，因此每一位持有者都面临续期节点——Netgear 与 Adtran 在 2027 年 10 月，eero 与 Nokia 在 2027 年 10 月，而按文章给出的日期，华硕在 2028 年 3 月。这使其成为一道会反复出现的关卡，而非一次性放行；这一点对本库所跟踪的产品路线图最为相关。

本条目的每一项要素都锤定在 FCC 自身的文件记录上：华硕的条目及其 2028 年 3 月 6 日的终止日期见于公告 DA 26-957 与已发布的受管制清单，路由器条目的清单原文、DoW 附条件批准机制，以及 Netgear、Adtran 与 eero 的批准及日期亦同。媒体报道只是使该批准进入视野的渠道，而非其成立的依据。
