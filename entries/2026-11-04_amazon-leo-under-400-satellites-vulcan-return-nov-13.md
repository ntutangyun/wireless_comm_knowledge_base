## Summary (EN)

SatNews (3 November 2026), crediting a SpaceNews report of the same date, says Amazon has fewer than 400 operational broadband satellites in orbit for its Amazon Leo constellation, whose full size is 3,232 satellites. Amazon originally contracted 77 heavy-lift missions across Arianespace, Blue Origin and ULA, and technical setbacks at those providers have constrained launch cadence.

The article says ULA's Vulcan has been grounded since February after a solid rocket booster nozzle anomaly during a Space Force mission, with national security flights reassigned to SpaceX. Vulcan may return to flight as early as 13 November from Space Launch Complex 41 with the Vulcan Leo 1 (LV-01) mission, the first operational flight of the VC6L configuration (six Northrop Grumman GEM 63XL boosters, Centaur V upper stage), carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites in a 5.4 m fairing. It also reports that Blue Origin's New Glenn exploded during a static-fire test in May, and that Ariane 6 has struggled to reach the flight frequency Amazon needs.

On regulatory milestones, the article says Amazon must deploy half the constellation by mid-decade and all 3,232 satellites by July 2029 under its FCC authorization, and cites analysts who see 2027 as the decisive year if launch rates ramp across all three rocket families. The article does not state a commercial service launch date or current service availability.
