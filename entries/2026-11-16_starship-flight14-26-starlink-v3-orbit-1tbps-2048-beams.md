## Summary (EN)

TelecomTV (29 Sep 2026) reports that on its fourteenth test flight, launched on Monday of that week, Starship reached orbit and released its payload, deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites; the article calls it the first time Starship had done both (Flight 13 in July had released satellites on a suborbital trajectory). Elon Musk wrote on X: "All 26 Starlink V3 operational satellites deployed and operating nominally." The article gives each V3 satellite 1 Tbit/s of downlink and 160 Gbit/s of uplink capacity, and quotes that "its antennas support 2,048 beams each way", against 192 downlink and 144 uplink beams on the V2 arrays. SpaceX expected service on the new satellites to begin within a few weeks, subject to testing.

The flight was shortened. One of the ship's three main engines shut down early, and SpaceX's mission summary says the flight control team "decided to limit the duration spent on orbit", cutting the orbital phase to about three hours instead of the planned ten. The ship made a controlled descent into the northern Pacific.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Payload: 26 operational Starlink V3 satellites; Musk reported all deployed and "operating nominally".
- Per-satellite capacity (TelecomTV): 1 Tbit/s downlink, 160 Gbit/s uplink.
- Phased arrays: 2,048 beams each way per satellite, versus 192 downlink / 144 uplink beams on V2.
- Service: expected to start within a few weeks, subject to testing.
- Flight anomaly: early shutdown of one of three ship main engines; orbital time cut from 10 h to about 3 h; controlled descent into the northern Pacific.
