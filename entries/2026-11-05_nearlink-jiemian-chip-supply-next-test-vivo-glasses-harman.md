## Summary (EN)

Jiemian News (界面新闻), republished on Sina Tech on 2026-10-29, reports from a media exchange event and an interview with the International SparkLink Alliance (iSLA) secretary-general Zeng Guosong (曾国松). Its headline argument is that, with adoption moving faster than the alliance expected, the next test is the chip supply chain. The piece overlaps with other CEIC2026-period coverage already in this knowledge base on the headline shipment figures (about 114 million chips in 2025; 150-200 million expected in 2026) and on the 1.6 million digital car key sets across 30+ models; this entry covers the parts that those entries do not.

The article sets SparkLink in the same category as Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, quotes the official claim of about 60% of Bluetooth's power use at about 6x the transfer rate, and notes the alliance has 1,260+ members (nearly 100 international this year). It then turns to supply. Zeng is quoted as saying the challenge comes from the demand side, that there are now 7 major chip suppliers, that most new generations of domestic chips reach commercial use next year or the year after, and that major overseas vendors are about two years away. The article names Maxscend (卓胜微), Telink (泰凌微) and Zhongke Jingshang (中科晶上) as developing SparkLink chips with supply starting next year, and says STMicroelectronics, NXP and MediaTek are key members, Qualcomm is in talks with the alliance, and Harman is negotiating to join.

For new categories, Zeng says SparkLink suits devices that Bluetooth struggles with, such as AI glasses needing continuous video and voice; the article gives vivo's AI glasses as an example that, per the article, were planned last year to use SparkLink but shipped with a wired connection because the SparkLink 2.0 chip was not yet available. Zeng also argues that Bluetooth must remain backward compatible so its stack keeps growing, whereas SparkLink assigns one standard per use case with versions 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 coexisting so that new devices can adopt the latest version. He expects over 8 billion short-range wireless connections in China by 2030, notes Bluetooth and Wi-Fi chips each ship 4-5 billion units a year worldwide, and the article relays Shanghai HiSilicon's projection of 1 billion SparkLink chips by 2030. For the car market, Zeng estimates about 30 million vehicles a year in China, of which about 18 million are NEVs, with about 70% of those fitted with digital keys (about 12 million vehicles), and says he is confident of capturing half. The article closes that automakers and appliance makers have validated the specification and that the supply chain will determine commercial strength.

## Key technical points (EN)

- **Supply (per Zeng)**: 7 major SparkLink chip suppliers today; most new domestic chip generations expected to be commercial next year or the year after; overseas majors about 2 years away. These are statements relayed by one outlet, not confirmations from the chip companies.
- **Named new chip developers (article)**: Maxscend, Telink and Zhongke Jingshang, with supply said to begin next year.
- **Alliance membership remarks**: ST, NXP and MediaTek described as key members; Qualcomm in talks; Harman negotiating to join (per Zeng, unconfirmed by the companies).
- **AI glasses example**: vivo's AI glasses planned (last year) for SparkLink but shipped wired because the SparkLink 2.0 chip was not available (article's example, attributed to Zeng).
- **Positioning claims**: about 60% of Bluetooth's power at about 6x the rate, and one standard covering parts of Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and UWB positioning (official claims as cited, not measured in the article).
- **Versioning argument (per Zeng)**: one standard per use case, versions 1.0/2.0/3.0 coexisting, versus Bluetooth's backward-compatibility burden.
- **Volume projections**: 1 billion SparkLink chips by 2030 (HiSilicon projection as relayed); 8 billion+ short-range connections in China by 2030 (Zeng); Bluetooth and Wi-Fi each 4-5 billion chips a year worldwide (Zeng).
- **Car market sizing (per Zeng)**: about 30 million vehicles a year, of which about 18 million NEVs, about 70% of those with digital keys (about 12 million vehicles), aim to capture half; UWB solution said to cost about 2,000 yuan versus about half that for SparkLink at similar accuracy.
