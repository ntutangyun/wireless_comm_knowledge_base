## Summary (EN)

A Hacker News thread (151 points, 152 comments, 2026-10-14) discusses a defence24.com article reporting that Starlink/Starshield terminals used by Ukrainian forces on drones lose connectivity once the platform moves faster than roughly 160 km/h, according to Ukrainian sources. The article adds that later reports claimed the threshold had been tightened, with some operators losing the link at 60-70 km/h; it describes these later reports as unconfirmed.

NBC News (2026-10-14) reports that Ukrainian officials accused SpaceX of limiting the service, that a spokesperson for President Zelenskyy said the limits were still in place late on 2026-10-14, and that Elon Musk wrote on social media that Starshield is owned and controlled by the US government and that "any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS". defence24 reports that a suggested link to US administration pressure (raised by Ukrainian commentator Serhii Sternenko) is unproven: SpaceX had not confirmed such a motive, and the White House had not confirmed ordering the change. NBC reports that the White House and SpaceX did not immediately respond to requests for comment. Most HN comments discuss the political context rather than the terminal behaviour.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Reported behaviour: connectivity on in-motion Starshield terminals fails above about 160 km/h (Ukrainian sources via defence24); a lower 60-70 km/h threshold is reported but unconfirmed.
- defence24 states this is not a general shutdown of Starlink for the Ukrainian military; it concerns terminals used in motion, especially under the Starshield military package.
- The link is used for command, video and mission updates beyond line of sight, according to the article; GNSS or inertial navigation is separate from this data link.
- The article concludes that the provider can remotely change terminal operating parameters; SpaceX CEO Elon Musk attributes the decision to the US military and the President (NBC); the White House has not confirmed ordering the change (defence24), and the stated reason for it has not been published.
- Primary reporting: NBC News and defence24.com, both 2026-10-14.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

No earlier KB entry records a speed-dependent limit on in-motion Starlink/Starshield terminals. The thread documents, through press reports, that, in defence24's words, "SpaceX can remotely change the operational parameters of terminals", while Musk says Starshield limits are set by the US government; the reported effect is on airborne use above a speed threshold (about 160 km/h, with lower thresholds reported but unconfirmed). defence24 notes that similar terminal verification and limiting mechanisms were used earlier in 2026 against Russian use of Starlink on drones. The cause, the exact thresholds, and whether the limits are still in force are not independently established in the sources read. This entry was sourced from the community thread; the primary reports cited above should be preferred for follow-up.
