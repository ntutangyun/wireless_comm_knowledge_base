## Summary (EN)

Via Satellite (Rachel Jewett, 3 Nov 2026) reports that AT&T and AST SpaceMobile demonstrated, in late October 2026, a handoff of a phone call from AT&T's terrestrial cellular network to AST SpaceMobile's direct-to-device satellite network when the phone left cellular coverage. The companies described it as a first-of-its-kind demonstration. Ericsson contributed radio and core technology and network automation to optimise handoffs on the AT&T side, and Nokia supplied radio access technology and mobility software.

AT&T's Brian Walsh compared the transition to going from a highway to a moving plane and said the connection worked "perfectly", with the handoff visible in engineering logs. AT&T's Robert Walters said the successful handoff "is a significant milestone, but it's not the finish line." The next phase is expected to be beta services under real-world conditions, with commercial service as the longer-term goal. The article gives AT&T's agreement with AST SpaceMobile as dating to 2024 and running as a revenue share through 2030, and says AST expects to begin initial commercial service with about 45 satellites in orbit, a number it expects to reach around early 2027. The article does not specify the spectrum, devices, satellites or the detailed handoff mechanism used in the test.

- Demonstrated: a voice call moved from AT&T's cellular network to AST's D2D satellite network when the phone left terrestrial coverage (late Oct 2026).

- Not disclosed in the article: spectrum, handset model, which BlueBird satellites were used, handoff trigger and signalling.
- Stated next step: beta service under real-world conditions; AST's initial commercial service is tied to about 45 satellites, expected around early 2027.
