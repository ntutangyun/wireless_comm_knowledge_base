## Summary (EN)
AST SpaceMobile's second-quarter 2026 business update (10 August 2026) is the first quarterly readout since the BlueBird 8–13 launches and puts numbers on how far the direct-to-device network is from a real service. The constellation now stands at **13 spacecraft in orbit with roughly 20,000 square feet of combined phased-array aperture** — six Block-2 satellites launched within 50 days (BlueBirds 8–10 in June, 11–13 on 5 August). BlueBirds 14–16 are described as ready to ship shortly and BlueBirds 17–46 are in various stages of production and assembly. On the ground, the company says an **initial 3,000 digital cells have been activated across the continental United States** as preparation for a 2026 **beta service** — scaled, non-commercial usage with select strategic mobile-operator partners in chosen markets — and that **nearly 50 gateways** are in various stages of completion, installation and planning ahead of service.

On radio performance the update repeats the Block-2 design targets rather than reporting measured on-orbit throughput: **peak data rates approaching 200 Mbps** per Block-2 satellite (Block 1 demonstrated nearly 100 Mbps), a proprietary ASIC with **up to 10 GHz of processing bandwidth per satellite** giving a claimed 10× throughput improvement over Block 1, and "AI-enabled spectrum management" credited with a further user-experience gain. The spectrum posture is stated as a target of **~100 MHz of access in the US and 60+ MHz globally on a market-by-market basis**, combining shared MNO low-band spectrum with AST-controlled MSS spectrum. Commercially: 60+ MNO partners covering 3+ billion subscribers, European integration work with Vodafone, Orange, Telefónica, Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone Ukraine, a US joint venture with the top three US MNOs, ~$1.3 billion of contracted revenue backlog, over $125 million of US Government awards, preliminary selection under Japan's J-LEO program (up to ~$1 billion of non-dilutive government capital, via the Rakuten joint venture), Q2 revenue of $31.5 million, ~$2.7 billion cash (June 30) and a reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guide of $150–200 million.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
The KB's AST thread so far records the Q1 readout (2026-05-24_ast-spacemobile-q1-2026-45-bluebirds), the FCC full-constellation approval (2026-05-30), the first Block-2 launch (2026-06-18), the commercial-service deferral to early 2027 (2026-07-22), Japan/Rakuten D2C going live (2026-08-05) and BlueBird 11–13 (2026-08-07). This Q2 update adds the first *network-side* activation metric — **3,000 digital cells across CONUS** — plus a gateway count (~50), which together describe the terrestrial half of the beta service; earlier entries only counted satellites. Two things worth reading carefully: (i) the throughput figures are still design targets ("expected to deliver ... approaching 200 Mbps"), so the awaited measured Block-2 on-orbit throughput readout flagged since June has not yet arrived; and (ii) the spectrum posture (~100 MHz US via shared MNO low-band plus controlled MSS) is now stated as a target rather than a holding, which sharpens the contrast with Starlink's owned EchoStar mid-band, Amazon Leo's filed D2D system and Equatys' licensed multi-market L/S-band (2026-08-14). Watch items: BlueBird 14–16 launch, the beta-service activation announcement, and any first field throughput numbers.
