## Summary (EN)

SatNews reports that on Friday, October 2, 2026, AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon announced a joint venture to pool industry resources and streamline direct-to-device (D2D) satellite connectivity across the United States. Paul Roth was named interim chief executive officer while the board conducts a search for a permanent leader.

According to the report, the joint venture operates as a collaborative platform rather than an independent satellite provider, so each carrier keeps its existing, separate commercial space agreements. AT&T and Verizon continue deployments with AST SpaceMobile, while T-Mobile maintains its direct-to-cell beta with SpaceX's Starlink constellation. The venture is described as developing a standardized approach to satellite integration in coordination with mobile operating system developers, OEMs and application creators, focused on optimizing supplemental coverage from space (SCS) protocols, expanding text, voice and data capabilities, and reducing latency during satellite handoffs. The article also says the carriers intend uniform standards for satellite-directed messaging and emergency alerting.

In a joint statement quoted by the article, the three partners said that through combined investment "provider options will expand, and, as a first step, direct-to-device (D2D) access will improve." The article does not give technical parameters, spectrum or a launch schedule for the venture.
