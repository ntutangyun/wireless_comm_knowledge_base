## Summary (EN)

The Register reported on 2026-11-24 that the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) will vote in December on proposals that would restrict foreign satellite operators in the same way their home countries restrict US providers. The FCC says the aim is to "ensure reciprocity and fair dealing" in satellite rules. Following an initial consultation in March, the December vote would open a rulemaking to reconsider how foreign-licensed operators gain access to the US market.

FCC chairman Brendan Carr is quoted as saying American providers face regulatory and spectrum limits that foreign competitors do not, that "It is time for this regulatory asymmetry to end", and that the notice proposes to apply "those or similar regulatory restraints" to satellite providers from countries that restrict American providers.

## Key technical points (EN)

- Source: The Register (Dan Robinson), 2026-11-24: https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/11/24/fcc-proposes-tit-for-tat-restrictions-on-foreign-satellite-operators/5311889. The article links the FCC draft (DOC-427492A1, PDF); the draft itself was not read for this entry.
- EU context named in the article: the FCC singled out the EU's Digital Networks Act, which proposes harmonised connectivity rules across the bloc; a separate EU proposal would overhaul authorisation for mobile satellite services (MSS) in the 2 GHz band after existing rights expire in May 2027. Earlier this year the FCC said EU-wide spectrum authorisation would replace national licensing that US operators are used to and could require relicensing of US systems operating in Europe.
- Analyst view quoted in the article (CCS Insight, Joe Gardiner): for two-thirds of the spectrum, only operators based in the EU, with EU ownership and aligned with the EU's strategic goals, will be considered, which he says excludes companies such as SpaceX and Amazon Leo.
- UK: the article says Britain is also a target; the FCC previously claimed the UK Space Agency allocated about 75% of its 2023/24 budget to European Space Agency (ESA) procurements, which US companies cannot bid for because the US is not an ESA member. If reciprocal measures are adopted, British satellite operators could face restrictions on US market access.
- The article does not mention Starlink by name, and does not specify which foreign spectrum limits would be mirrored.
