## Summary (EN)

At SpaceX's IPO roadshow on June 26–27, 2026, President and COO Gwynne Shotwell disclosed that SpaceX is considering launching a Starlink-branded retail mobile phone service for US consumers — billing subscribers directly rather than staying a wholesale capacity provider behind partners like T-Mobile — and may eventually build its own terrestrial cellular network to support it. This is the clearest signal yet that SpaceX intends to compete head-on with AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile rather than complement them.

The foundation is the EchoStar spectrum acquisition the FCC approved in May 2026: $17 billion for 65 MHz of mid-band spectrum in the 1.9–2.2 GHz range — 40 MHz of AWS-4, 15 MHz of unpaired AWS-3, and 10 MHz of H-Block — with the transfer expected to complete by November 2027. Exclusive, contiguous mid-band spectrum removes the biggest constraint on Starlink's first-generation direct-to-cell service (which piggybacked on T-Mobile's PCS spectrum) and enables wider-bandwidth satellite 5G claimed to support a capacity increase of more than 100× the first-generation D2D system.

The sober counterweights: 65 MHz is small against the ~1,020 MHz the big three carriers collectively hold; satellite links cannot match terrestrial density in urban areas, so a credible retail offer would need ground infrastructure SpaceX does not yet have (hence the "may build its own terrestrial network" hedge). Oppenheimer analysts project ~15 million US Starlink subscribers by 2030. The disclosure reframes the D2D landscape: what began as coverage-gap filling ("no dead zones") is now positioned as a fourth-carrier play anchored in orbital capacity economics.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

The KB's direct-to-cell thread previously recorded the FCC *denying* SpaceX's new D2C spectrum bid while authorizing more Gen2 satellites — the regulatory posture then was "coverage supplement, not carrier." The May EchoStar approval plus the June roadshow disclosure flips that trajectory: exclusive mid-band satellite spectrum, retail billing, and a possible terrestrial layer describe a full MNO strategy, not supplemental coverage. Watch items: the FCC transfer completion (Nov 2027), how the 100× Gen2 capacity claim materializes, and whether the terrestrial-network hedge becomes a buildout or a spectrum-hosting deal with an existing carrier.
