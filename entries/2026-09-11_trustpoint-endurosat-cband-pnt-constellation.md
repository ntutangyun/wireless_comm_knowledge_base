## Summary (EN)
TrustPoint, a commercial positioning-navigation-timing (PNT) provider, announced on 2026-09-10 a manufacturing contract with EnduroSat to build 40 additional LEO satellites, delivered across three production tranches, growing the company's constellation to 47 spacecraft. Payload Space reports three demo satellites already launched (two early tech-demos in 2023 and a full-tech-stack demo in 2025); a companion Via Satellite report of the same date adds that the 47 total comprises those three previously launched spacecraft plus four in development under a SpaceWERX Tactical Funding Increase (TACFI) awarded earlier in 2026. The satellites are small (roughly 10 kg each) and low-cost (a few hundred thousand dollars apiece), keeping the whole build well under the cost of a traditional GNSS constellation. TrustPoint's differentiator is the choice of C-band rather than the L-band used by GPS/GNSS: C-band carries fewer regulatory burdens than the crowded L-band spectrum and is claimed to be more resistant to ionospheric disturbance and solar-storm-driven degradation than GPS's L-band signals, while the LEO altitude (versus GPS's MEO) and tighter footprint enable more targeted regional service. First launch is targeted for no earlier than 2027, and TrustPoint CEO Patrick Shannon said he expects the full constellation could come online in the next 24 to 30 months, subject to supply chain and launch availability. Payload Space also notes TrustPoint's "first real-time demonstration of a C-band GNSS signal".

## Key technical points (EN)
- **Constellation:** 40 new satellites (3 tranches) added to 3 already-launched demo satellites (two 2023 tech-demos plus a 2025 full-stack demo) and 4 in development = 47-satellite total; manufacturer EnduroSat. The 47-total breakdown and the TACFI attribution come from Via Satellite, not from the primary cited report.
- **Band:** C-band (vs. GPS/GNSS's L-band) — chosen for lower regulatory burden and claimed better resistance to ionospheric and solar-storm-driven signal degradation.
- **Orbit / mass:** LEO (closer than GPS's MEO); ~10 kg per satellite; unit cost in the low hundreds-of-thousands-of-dollars range.
- **Schedule:** first launch NET 2027; per CEO Patrick Shannon the full constellation could come online in the next 24-30 months, subject to supply chain and launch availability.
- **Prior funding:** per Via Satellite, 4 of the constellation's satellites are in development under a SpaceWERX Tactical Funding Increase (TACFI) awarded earlier in 2026.
- **Roadmap:** per Via Satellite, CEO Patrick Shannon said TrustPoint plans to double its order for successive build-outs, potentially looking at 80, then 160 more satellites.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
This adds a second named entrant to the KB's regulatory-side LEO-PNT / GNSS-alternative thread that began with 2026-08-28_xona-pulsar-fcc-full-constellation-authorization (Xona Space Systems, L-band adjacent to GPS, FCC-authorized 258-satellite constellation). TrustPoint's C-band approach differs architecturally from GPS's L-band allocation, avoiding direct spectral adjacency to GPS/GNSS, and pursues a much smaller, cheaper satellite design (~10 kg vs. dedicated PNT-class buses) manufactured by a smallsat specialist (EnduroSat) rather than a traditional prime. Unlike the Xona entry, this is a manufacturing-contract announcement rather than a spectrum/regulatory authorization — no FCC filing or licence detail is disclosed in the source reporting, which is flagged here as an open question for a future entry once a licensing action is filed or granted.

本条目为知识库中始于 2026-08-28_xona-pulsar-fcc-full-constellation-authorization（Xona Space Systems，采用与 GPS 相邻的 L 波段，获 FCC 授权建设 258 颗卫星星座）的低轨 PNT／GNSS 替代方案监管侧脉络，增添了第二个具名参与者。TrustPoint 的 C 波段方案在架构上与 GPS 的 L 波段分配不同，避免了与 GPS/GNSS 的频谱直接相邻，并采用体积更小、成本更低的卫星设计（约 10 千克，远小于专用 PNT 级卫星平台），由小卫星专业制造商 EnduroSat 承建，而非传统大型主承包商。与 Xona 条目不同，本次公告属于制造合同宣布，而非频谱/监管授权——现有报道未披露 FCC 备案或许可细节，本条目将其标记为待后续条目跟进的公开问题，待相关许可行动提交或获批后再补充。
