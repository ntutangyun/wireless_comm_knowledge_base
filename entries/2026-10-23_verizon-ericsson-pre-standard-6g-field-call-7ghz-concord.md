## Summary (EN)

In a press release dated 15 October 2026, Ericsson and Verizon announced that they completed what the release calls "the first pre-standard 6G field call in the 7 GHz" band, at a Verizon commercial network site in Concord, California. The release describes a fully deployed Ericsson 6G proof-of-concept kit: a base station installed in the site shelter, and user equipment housed in a small house on wheels, roughly 12 by 8 feet, with dedicated power and battery backup and varied wall and window materials, so that both stationary outdoor and indoor radio configurations can be tested. The carrier bandwidth was 400 MHz.

According to the release, the confirmed field result is an outdoor call; the broader proof-of-concept programme examines "outside-in penetration through different glass and building materials", and coverage, throughput, latency, stability and feature behaviour across line-of-sight and non-line-of-sight paths, foliage and obstacles. The release gives no throughput, latency or distance figures and names no specific Ericsson product; the stack is described as pre-standard. Both companies state that this is a proof-of-concept demonstration and not a commercial 6G service launch, and that analysis of the field results will continue. Yago Tenorio (Verizon CTO and SVP of Technology Development) is quoted on Ericsson's role as a founding member of the Verizon 6G Innovation Forum; Hannes Ekström (Head of Customer Unit Verizon, Ericsson) is quoted on the field learnings that "can help turn the promise of 6G into deployable technology".

The "world's first" / "global first" wording is the companies' own claim, relayed here as such.
