## Summary (EN)
Open Cosmos (Oxfordshire, UK) announced on 2 September 2026 that the UK Government Commercial Agency (GCA) has selected it as an officially certified and vetted provider under the **TacSys** (Tactical Communication System) framework, a Ministry of Defence procurement vehicle valued at up to **£8 billion** and running through **June 2034**. The framework is the vehicle through which private-sector satellite-connectivity technology gets integrated into MoD-owned communications capabilities. Open Cosmos will offer its **ConnectedCosmos** constellation: a described "multi-layer" LEO architecture that is **gatewayless**, using **optical inter-satellite links (ISL)** so that data "routes directly between satellites without ever touching the ground," bypassing subsea cables and terrestrial infrastructure the announcement characterises as potentially compromised or hostile. The architecture combines **point-to-point broadband** with **direct-to-device IoT sensing** in a single orbital framework, intended to deliver real-time threat detection and actionable, space-derived intelligence within a "closed, secure loop." The announcement frames the selection around Open Cosmos's satellite-design and mission-delivery track record meeting MoD criteria for interoperability, rapid deployment, and national space sovereignty. The source material available at scan time did not disclose a satellite count, orbital-capacity or bandwidth figures, or a deployment timeline beyond the framework's 2034 end date.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)
This knowledge base's sat-iot and leo-constellations bins have tracked D2D-IoT and broadband as largely separate system designs across different operators. Open Cosmos's framing is notable for treating them as one orbital framework from the outset — a single gatewayless, optical-ISL constellation carrying both direct-to-device sensing traffic and point-to-point broadband — sold specifically as a defense/government sovereign-connectivity capability under a long-running (through 2034), large-value UK procurement framework. It is the first entry in this KB for Open Cosmos and for the TacSys framework, and adds a UK sovereign-defense data point to the growing list of "gatewayless, optical-mesh LEO for government/defense use" announcements this bin has been accumulating (e.g. the Telesat/Cailabs optical-ISL government item logged the same week).
