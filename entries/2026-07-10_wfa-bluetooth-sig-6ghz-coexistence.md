## Summary (EN)

Wi-Fi Alliance and the Bluetooth Special Interest Group announced (June 19, 2026; Austin, TX and Kirkland, WA) that they are **initiating formal joint work on fair coexistence between Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, with an initial focus on the 6 GHz band**. The stated driver is the growth of high-performance use cases and device density across both ecosystems; the two organizations will jointly identify practical mechanisms and evaluate coexistence across unlicensed spectrum bands.

The announcement is short on protocol specifics but heavyweight in signaling: it is the first formal, standing coexistence program between the two bodies, and its 6 GHz focus effectively confirms that **Bluetooth's expansion into the 6 GHz band** (under study in the Bluetooth SIG's higher-band work) is close enough to require an inter-SDO framework before devices ship. Wi-Fi Alliance CEO Kevin Robinson framed the work as "responsible spectrum stewardship" across nearly 10 billion combined annual device shipments; Bluetooth SIG CEO Neville Meijers emphasized richer experiences for an expanding wireless ecosystem.

## Key technical points (EN)

- First formal **joint coexistence program between Wi-Fi Alliance and Bluetooth SIG**; announced June 19, 2026.
- **Initial focus: the 6 GHz band** — anticipating Bluetooth operation alongside Wi-Fi 6E/7/8 in 5925–7125 MHz.
- Scope: identify practical coexistence mechanisms and evaluate coexistence across unlicensed bands (2.4 GHz implications included by extension).
- Combined footprint cited: ~10 billion devices shipped per year across the two technologies.
- No technical deliverables or timelines announced yet — a framework/workstream launch.

## Why it matters / what's new (EN)

2.4 GHz Wi-Fi/Bluetooth coexistence has been handled for two decades by in-device vendor mechanisms (packet traffic arbitration) without a standing inter-SDO program — the fact that 6 GHz gets one *before* Bluetooth ships in the band is new. For the KB's 6 GHz thread (2026-05-21_fcc-6ghz-rule-changes, 2026-05-04_eu-rspg-upper-6ghz-decision, 2026-07-10_south-africa-standard-power-6ghz-afc), this adds a *third technology* competing for the band beyond Wi-Fi and licensed mobile. It also matters for the IEEE 802 Coex SC (see the Mentor digests): expect ETSI BRAN and 802.11 coexistence documents to start referencing Bluetooth-in-6-GHz scenarios. For Bluetooth readers, this is the strongest public signal yet that the SIG's higher-band project is real and nearing productization.
